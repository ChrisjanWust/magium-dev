# Unit B3-U5 — Book 3, Chapter 5 (both routes)

## Unit focus

Both routes of chapter 5 (~26.4k words) and **the longest-scened pair in the early book**:
`b3ch5a` averages **~579 prose words per scene** (27 scenes), `b3ch5b` **~514** (21 scenes). Both are
far above project average.

**Primary target — P7, white-room dialogue.** This is your defining assignment. Very long scenes are
where floating-head exposition lives: extended debate and lore delivery with no terrain, no weather,
no objects, no bodies. With only 48 scenes across both files, you can work every single one.

For each scene ask: **could this be performed on a bare stage?** If yes, stage it. Anchor conversation
in camp chores, weapon maintenance, injury, food preparation, weather, the specific feel of ground
underfoot, posture, hands. Two committed Book 1 calibration passages are in Master Contract §7 — the
stream-water beat and the wintergreen-salve beat. Both are conversations that became scenes because
the characters were doing something physical while talking.

Do not solve this by appending scenery paragraphs. Interleave the physical business **through** the
dialogue so the action carries subtext.

contract for what each value means emotionally). `b3ch5a` also reads `v_b3_ch2_goblins`;
`b3ch5b` **sets `v_b3_ch5_fight`**, read downstream in `b3ch6a` and `b3ch6b`.

Note the asymmetry: `5b` makes a decision that shapes chapter 6, `5a` does not. Record precisely what
each `v_b3_ch5_fight` value means in your `CONTINUITY EXIT STATE` — unit B3-U6 depends on it.
