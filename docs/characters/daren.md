# Daren

## Overview

Daren is a mid-forties white-mage healer and traveling hero, known across the eastern continent as the healer in armor. He enters the Varathia tournament for the Magium: not as a prize in itself, but as the only way he can see to save more people than a single swordsman can reach on the road. He talks like a legend, then undercuts himself with laughter, sarcasm, or embarrassment. He treats protection as a vow, healing as a duty that outranks mood or politics, and mercy as a matter of gods and honor—until a victim he cannot save breaks that frame.

## Appearance

Bald, dark-skinned, and tall, with an X-shaped scar on his forehead from two cuts by his old martial and enchanting master. He skips a helmet so he can keep peripheral vision and so people recognize him. In the field he wears heavy plate and fights with sword and shield; in camp he changes into more comfortable clothes. His healing shows as visible white light and works only when he is calm. Enchanting puts him in a yellow-eyed trance.

The plate is compensation, not style. He never mastered the physical half of a shield spell, and ordinary protections against physical hits barely work, so armor and enchanted weapons have to carry that load. The sword is his real toolkit: purification, element absorption and return, and a cut that can sever a mage’s link to the magical plane. The shield is his default defense and can hold sleep wards and large barriers. He later learns to move and recall the blade by aura, glue it to his palm, and cut or cancel aura “knots.” After a soul-body misalignment is corrected, he can fight without the armor’s weight.

## Personality and tendencies

He defaults to leader: routes, watches, gear, public diplomacy, after-action coaching. He will deny the title and ask for unanimous votes, then still speak for the group. He plans first—conserve mage energy, stay together, hide tracks, use rules as weapons—and still charges a stronger enemy on principle rather than let cruelty walk. Survival and protection matter more to him than tournament standing or spectacle.

The heroic register is real and unstable. Life-debts produce instant loyalty, oversharing, and “Adventure awaits!” energy that collapses into jokes. He trusts quickly after a moral act and sorts people as good or not. He is open about his own weaknesses and shrugs when others will not share theirs. He talks most on the road, needles to defuse tension, and recovers that needling as soon as a crisis eases. Pride delays amends; when he finally decides he was wrong, the apology is awkward and formal.

White-mage dogma outruns evidence for a long time: women cannot be born mages; a strong unexplained aura is a delayed banshee; purification is mercy. He measures himself by promises to protect, not by honor-talk about glorious deaths. He needs someone to save in order to function. When that fails he withdraws, goes cold, or turns lethal. He will not finish a helpless enemy if he can bind them and leave fate to the gods, and he hates being the first to betray an ally. Idle rule-breaking and unnecessary kills grate on him. He over-stacks protections, under-lists them, and treats medical work as personal responsibility he will try to carry alone until the party refuses.

He holds childhood ideals his old master calls naive and will not become a sage’s henchman. Magium theory leaves him self-deprecating and hungry for a usable model: knowledge that means something for healing, without the politics. Once advice proves real, the half-hearted trainee becomes a pestering student who learns in concrete images—knots, glue, reverse the steps.

## Reactions and behavior

Threat to a companion → intercept the blow, drop weapons if they are hostage, hang on a breaking beam and lie that he has it under control so no one climbs down after him. Split from the group → safety lecture aimed at the others, not himself.

Visible cruelty he cannot legally stop → fist-shaking rage, then talk and money before any shortcut. Persuasion failing into murder → collapse, then immediate lethal force once the block is gone. A body he could not save → chest pain he never gets used to, stacked heals, self-blame, or walking past the person who tries to comfort him.

An apparent stillwater, god, or banshee → challenge first. He will shout and charge Eiden after repeated humiliations, refuse to kneel to a god after a lethal demonstration, and start a purification ritual on a “banshee problem” before he has the facts. He distinguishes a mage he thinks he understands from a literal god, and later distinguishes cutting someone off from the magical plane from killing them. That distinction can cost him the window to finish a fight.

Defeated enemies → spare and bind unless argued into the cost of escape. Assassination-for-hire → halt, unless the target is framed as a tyrant and sealed with a blood oath. Teammates ignoring “stay behind me” → a deep sigh, then he holds the line anyway. Party tension → “Whoa, whoa,” then a three-person deal. Out-argued → grumbles and complies. Caught whispering → a terrible cover story.

He uses theater when force will not work: a public surrender and a joke at a friend’s expense to keep a crowd from demanding a death. He is unusually calm walking into a forced duel and short with delays once the threat is gone. Quarantine, plague, and wounded civilians pull him off the Magium clock. A revenant wearing a dead friend’s face → deny the name, reach for the sword, yield only when killing is futile.

## Relationships

**Barry.** The stranger who saved him becomes debtor, shield, and would-be partner. Daren overshares his greatest weakness, covers for him in public, enchants his gear, and lectures him for scavenging poorly or splitting the party. A concealed Magium motive feels like betrayal; he goes cold on the road and still heals, fights, and will hand over his sword if asked. He looks to Barry first after a trial that shames him, relies on him as brake and sounding board, and breaks morally if Barry treats captives’ lives as negotiable.

**Kate.** He grants her escort on reputation, then watches her as a delayed banshee he has promised—reluctantly—not to purify until she transforms. He does not grasp why someone that strong asked for protection, and for a long stretch he does not look at her as human. Trust-breaking violence from her produces a combat-first purification attempt that still yields to a stated promise and still ends in healing. After her Beacon past comes out he freezes her out, then offers a late, strained apology she barely registered as a crisis. Later he trusts her with the battle plan, accepts her ice-dome rest against his keep-moving instinct, and credits her when she copies his aura work or drops his floating sword.

**Rose.** Default protectee. He overrides consultation to get a sick child a cure, lifts her off monsters, and treats her death as a failed escort vow. If she lives, he keeps a transceiver link and still insists she is not a mage who can fix the world. A revenant in her shape is not her: he refuses the name and any alliance until forced.

**Hadrik.** Adventure-story rivalry and a personality clash he will admit over ale. He denies any feud, goes acid when teased about the armor, and explodes if Hadrik eulogizes a death as honorable. They search, spar, and drink together; Daren stays a slow, controlled drinker and will make a one-time exception to a no-day-drinking rule when a friend needs company more than a lecture. He follows immediately when Hadrik storms out of a meeting, fearing a faction fight.

**Flower and Illuna.** First meeting is a purification attempt that fails because Illuna is already awake. He would rather risk killing Flower than leave a banshee unbound, nearly duels Illuna, and refuses to read a life-debt as friendship. He still demands both of them back if they are taken, heals Illuna when she hides a wound, and later suggests asking them about Magium nodes.

**Arraka.** He charges her on principle and loses in seconds. Distrust of her as coach gives way to free-time practice, kneeling for plague hints, and an expectation that she contribute. He still finds her voice irritating and cuts past primate insults to the question.

**Eiden.** Named an idealist and crushed for it; Daren treats him as an enemy to stop, not a savior. He will not take “free” power while a victim can still be talked out of danger, then begs for it when she is already dead—and again in a loop that forces him to recreate that failure. Hatred reads the power-offer as humiliation. By the end he argues that Eiden might still want to believe in humanity, holds a killing cut too long, and still fights when a companion is the target.

**Melindra.** Oath-holder and wary of her Eiden ties. He wants her treated as a teammate, not a hired gun; threatens to cancel the unsigned side of a deal if she sits out a lethal ambush; and will not cast a limiter on a stillwater aura. He frames keeping her out of ordinary fights as the safe call, rebukes her panic and teasing, and offers help finding her brother.

**Sophie.** A childhood marriage he hid for years, including from the party. He offered divorce, then an open marriage; she refused both and waits. He keeps her off Varathia and off the worst news because the information would only cause nightmares, then the same “too dangerous for you” line when fear comes through the transceiver. Once he can reach her, the calls become frequent, and she plans to travel with the group later.

**Nolderan.** Scar-giver, enchanting and martial teacher, old two-man timing still intact. The reunion is a hug, then the discovery that the master now serves a sage. Betrayal lands as “my old master truly is dead.” Daren will not be recruited; he can fight the man without collapsing into sentiment.

**Kelrim.** First willing partnership with someone he had classed as a monster. They diagnose and treat a plague together. Daren forbids a suicidal cast, will not leave him behind, and carries the body if the prohibition fails.

**Leila.** Heals her without ceremony and tells Kate to trust her in a fight instead of tunnel-visioning.

**Azarius.** Enough of a “great hero” that Azarius will delay to watch him. Daren thanks him later as someone who had everything to lose.

## History

Already famous as the savior of the eastern continent, Daren comes to Varathia for the Magium and binds himself to Barry after a humiliating loss that Barry talks him out of. He recruits Kate, escorts Rose home, and keeps charging Eiden after being put to sleep for it. White-mage certainty about Kate and then about Flower and Illuna produces silence, failed rituals, and delayed apologies. In Thilias, a public slave-beating becomes the break: he tries talk and money before any stillwater shortcut, and the outcome—saved or not—stays the measure he uses on himself.

He had already been married to Sophie in secret. In the arena he protects first, refuses a forced killing, and cites law against execution for not murdering a friend. He takes Melindra on under oath against a tyrant, reports coming calamity because cities should be warned even if fortifying is useless, and refuses his old master’s offer to serve a sage. A god’s trial makes him replay the street he could not fix.

In Ollendor he speaks at gates, breaks quarantine to treat an “impossible” plague, and learns aura work as physical images. He chairs Magium talk he only half understands, hates being first to betray an ally, and still shifts when the evidence is assassination rather than a bribe. He reaches Sophie, sheds the plate once his soul-body link is realigned, and goes into the last fight with Eiden willing to spare a man who might still change—and unwilling to let that hope become inaction when someone is choking in front of him.
