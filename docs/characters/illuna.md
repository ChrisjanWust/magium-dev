# Illuna

## Overview

Illuna is an elven spirit—locally called a banshee—who shares a body with Flower. They are separate souls fused for about twenty years, not a single personality with two moods. When Illuna holds the body, the face goes colder and the eyes turn bright blue. In the ether she has her own form; on the earthen plane she usually stays in Flower’s mind and pilots the shared body, linked telepathically even when they think without words.

She is the pair’s strategist and jailor of Arraka: dry, mission-first, and unwilling to perform warmth. Others know the massacre title attached to her name; for years she hid as “Petal.” She does not treat the party as friends even after she has saved them. Care shows as planning, healing, and vetoes, not as hospitality—that is Flower’s job.

## Appearance

On the shared body, control is obvious: blue eyes, a colder expression, and an aura she keeps fully suppressed. She can hold that composure through most crises; violent fluctuation is rare. She hides her age the way Arraka taught her.

In the ether she is herself: late twenties, blue hair and eyes, pointed ears, a blue dress, about Barry’s height, and a near-constant frown. Flower’s earthen aura goes faint when Flower sleeps. After Flower has overused the body, Illuna inherits every strained bone and muscle and may not be able to stand.

## Personality and tendencies

Illuna is precise, suspicious, and anti-small-talk. She frames people as temporary allies, not friends, and will not bid for trust with a handshake or a speech. She speaks when a scene is factually wrong—an empty market, a bad illusion, a misread corpse—and prefers analysis and a coordinated exit over a charge.

Combat is control first: decoy strength, cages, pins, then a taught technique. She prepares large water constructs early, spends scarce magic on protection before rejoining a fight, and still misapplies old Arraka lessons under pressure. She uses Flower’s athletics and reputation when the job requires it, then still draws a line “for Flower’s sake.” Stealth often means keeping Flower asleep. She teaches aura suppression, sleep, and water with structured, reliability-first lessons.

Grumpiness is her default care. Harsh phrasing is how she shows concern; Flower translates it as such. She ends conversations by going to sleep, parses her own wording instead of apologizing, and hides injury so the group can keep moving. She designs fail-safes. She treats Arraka as a sensor and irritant, not a peer, and will close the amulet when Arraka clowns, stalls, or whines.

Protective discipline toward Flower is the through-line. Illuna coaches breathing, refuses to wake Flower for a fire trap until pressed, will not dual-cast in a way that wrecks Flower’s instinctive flow if she can avoid it, and will spend a daily cage or teleport to shield someone else before she returns to the fight. That same discipline once froze Flower’s aging when Illuna entered the body; she still argues that treating Flower as an adult would be a lie, even after she admits the guilt and bakes an apology cake.

She loses composure almost only about Arraka, and she treats the Magium as something you do not challenge on its ground. She will not relax after a Magium fragment nearly ends the world, and she will not accept “it wasn’t really the Magium” as comfort.

## Reactions and behavior

- Small talk or a demand that she prove she is trustworthy → she cuts it off. No handshake, no performance.
- Flower blurting a social disaster → a physical facepalm, no cover story.
- A missing tell, an empty street, a delayed curse, a walking corpse → pause, correct the model, name the fact.
- Arraka stalling, hiding a sage, or playing mentor → frown, amulet shut, or an order to warn; she still bargains for maps and ambush calls.
- Arraka about to name the Magium, or claiming a near-omnicide was only a fragment → anger, fear, then prosecution. She grabs the amulet and demands a stop.
- Someone calling Arraka part of the team → she explodes: Arraka will not change; Illuna cites her own past mistake in trusting her.
- Combat that can be pinned → water yank, cage, drowning sphere timed to an inhale; cold instruction to accept it.
- A wounded stranger Barry should not approach, or a hostage situation that needs talking → she grabs the arm or the knife wrist, then lets go with a warning.
- Offered friendship, peacemaking, or a hug → suspicion, a flustered freeze, or a snap of “No.”
- A name, age, or “grown-up” charge about Flower → shock, a dark look, then a hair-split: she never called Flower a problem; Flower is fine the way she is.
- Her own guilt named as the real motive → a frown, then “I will think about it.”
- Magium, Eiden, or a stillwater she cannot beat head-on → she wants weaknesses, ceremony vs snitching, and no pointless assault. She will still follow an unspoken plan if she trusts the caster.
- Healing a wound that turns out unreal → she stops. She will heal first.

## Relationships

**Flower.** Separate will in one body: Flower fronts rescues, warmth, and food spells; Illuna handles water, cages, banshee politics, and the plan. Illuna is exasperated caretaker and strategist, not Flower’s inner voice. She puts Flower in stasis when stealth or a ruse requires it, pulls Flower out of crueler loops, checks Flower before answering anyone else after a crisis, and nags the red-eye form. She could refuse their daily tea ritual and does not. Distinct vetoes stay hers—ale, scouting, energy that is moving too fast. The aging freeze is her guilt; the cake and the “not an adult” concession are how she tries to live with it without rewriting the rule.

**Arraka.** Former mentor who groomed her, then broke her. Illuna hates her, needs her maps and sense, and still iterates on spells Arraka taught (cage, drowning, later a more stable water sphere). She is jailor, student, and the one person Arraka’s Magium talk can still scare into silence. After that scare there is a thin new obedience both ways, never trust. Illuna ignores “I raised you,” hands the amulet off with escape warnings, and will not reopen the alliance no matter how useful the coaching. Arraka’s killings and jokes are Arraka’s; Illuna’s job is to contain them.

**Daren.** Mutual kill-readiness at first, then a silent even-debt after she takes a death curse meant for him and he breaks it. Both reject Flower’s claim that they are friends. She calls him the oaf, tutors him on auras, and still goes when he says not to kill the guards. She will not let “Arraka is on the team” pass.

**Rose.** The first person she somewhat trusts: she refuses the handshake, later apologizes, and will sleep only under Kate or Rose’s watch. She will cast support on a revenant when the next trial requires it, without confusing that entity with Rose.

**Barry.** Useful, not a friend—until usefulness thickens into something she will not name. She vouches him, warns him, answers only if he targets her, punctures his bluffs, and later follows his unspoken attack plan. She stares him down when he slips; she lets him needle the Flower argument and will not let it become a three-way talk.

**Kate.** Willing teacher once asked; later the one who supplies the cold reading Kate cannot hold, and a partner in freeze-then-serpent work. Past courtesy. Illuna accepts being called harsh on Flower without changing the rule.

**Hadrik.** First healed, then kept at logistics distance. She tells him not to advertise a prize that makes them a target.

**Leila.** Polite, disinterested, no handshake; she still heals bruises, dumps water on her to wake her, and treats Leila and Reidzek’s plan to live in a city as a reason that city matters.

**Melindra.** Illuna probes a sudden “change of heart,” then spends a cage to shield her. Melindra covers Illuna’s follow and cites her aura suppression as the model. A joke that Illuna would kill them if they needed a second rescue gets “That is correct.”

**Fyron.** Old alliance without trust: information for kills and covert help. Nodes and secret doors are useful; he is not a friend.

**Eiden.** Twenty-year acquaintance who greets the massacre title, not Flower’s name. Illuna’s flash of fear is what stops Flower from attacking him. She addresses him when he appears, reads his spectacles as ceremony more than snitching, and will try a drowning sphere on him if the plan requires it—apprehensive if asked too early.

**Eleya.** Acknowledgment that Illuna has been a trustworthy ally gets a “Hmph,” not warmth.

## History

Arraka mentored her, then used that bond to reshape her. The sacred-woods massacre is the name ogres still use; Illuna hid as Petal for years after fusing with Flower. Eiden already knew the pair.

She meets Barry’s group as the colder half of the shared body: heals, plans, and will not sell trust. To keep a shaman from reading the party’s shock she puts Flower in stasis, cages them, and plays traitor—then maps a fort with Arraka, kills on the way in, saves Daren from a drop, and takes a death curse meant for him. She agrees, nervously, to travel on, and stops Flower from charging Eiden.

She keeps Flower offstage for stealth work, sabotages an arena path with Fyron, and fights with water, cage, and a drowning technique Arraka taught. She shuts Arraka after the banshee’s brief freedom, names delayed curses and revenants for what they are, and will not let Arraka treat Magium knowledge as a joke. In later trials she reenacts her own worst memory without much display, then pulls Flower out of Olmnar’s loop.

On the road and in Ollendor she stays the planner: aura lessons, secret doors, injury hidden, night watches insisted upon. She admits guilt over Flower’s frozen aging, bakes a cake, and still will not call Flower an adult. She holds Arraka to account when a Magium fragment hunts them. In the ether she teaches water as reliability, pilots Flower’s body rather than strain it, and refuses to recast Arraka as a teammate. By the last confrontations she is iterating Arraka’s drowning spell into something more stable, following a fight plan she does not need spoken, and remaining a distinct will in the body she shares—allied, not merged.
