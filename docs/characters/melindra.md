# Melindra

## Overview

Melindra is a stillwater air mage and half-lessathi who joins the party in Book 2 as a guide toward Ollendor. She looks about nineteen and is more than six hundred years old. She does not need friends, and she says so; she needs the job done, her secrets kept, and her stillwater image intact. Shared curiosity (rocks, half-lessathi blood, practical magic) is how she actually attaches. Teasing, pouting, and theatrical cling are her default cover. Loyalty shows up in crises, in teaching, and in who she will not help kill—not in warmth on demand.

## Appearance

Cute face; light-brown hair in a high ponytail; slim frame. She wears custom silk that looks like pajamas as daylight clothes and has done so for centuries, including when she was banished. The smile she uses in public is often fake; the mocking “darling” is the same register. When she means something, her face goes unusually serious.

Her aura reads as ordinary human, better hidden than Kate’s or Flower’s. She can suppress it completely. Wind is her element: levitation, flight currents, barriers, silencing, tornadoes, sharpened blades. Fine control is incomplete; overuse brings headaches, stray cyclones, and energy crash. She also has an energizing trick and an incomplete once-a-day invisibility in Eiden’s style. She collects rocks and still carries stones from Eiden’s old prison cell.

## Personality and tendencies

Independence is the spine. She takes shortcuts over roads and law, trades rather than gifts, and will not join a fight or a watch just to look like a teammate. Early on the party are tools toward a king and a stolen item. She withholds plans until she can demonstrate them. She leaves cleanly when her own goal is in reach and keeps the door open by transceiver, not by travelling with them.

Teasing is how she handles almost everyone. She pokes stereotypes, fills silence with romance needling, nicknames Kate “icey,” clings to Barry as theater, and needles Daren when his plans are empty or when he almost hugs someone. Pouting sits next to stillwater pride in the same scene. If she thinks someone “gets” the joke, she will refuse to tone it down. Petty payback (bugs, biscuits) is what rejection looks like, not a rewritten personality.

Loyalty is situational and slow. She helps people who have already helped her, and she helps Flower, without becoming soft. Once she has chosen a side in a real fight, she stops holding back. She will miss the group; she is bad at saying so unless someone else goes first. Eiden remains a friend as a person even when the party’s Magium bargain would make him a target.

Secrecy is survival. Still-winter history, her wind-control problem, the shape of a ruse, the exile’s stolen object, her brother, and the name of a contact all stay closed until demonstration, accident, or someone else forces the word. She would rather look like a perfect stillwater than admit a flaw. She is discreet about other people’s attachments.

She is a practical historian of stillwaters, prophecies, and old lessathi work, and a sensor for auras the rest of the group miss. She teaches by demonstration: rocks as an adulthood lesson, concealment drills, ice-aura sharpening, air by eye-path when her own magic will not fire. She is anti-splitting and anti-honor charges. Lethal convenience is a real option for her until someone she has sworn to binds her out of it. Socially she can be tone-deaf (race labels, “I wasn’t talking to you”) and still become the person who coaches, pathfinds, and nags the group into hiding.

She frames attachment as a policy problem: she outlives people. Optional closeness with Barry is still “not a relationship” in her mouth. That policy and her actual trust in Flower and Barry run in parallel; they are not the same thing.

## Reactions and behavior

Interrupted or doubted, she irritates, pouts, and may demand a spar that she will not rest after even when she nearly faints. Tickled or mocked, she remembers and withholds. Caught in a party huddle while she is on monster-watch, she calls it out; private talk is fine at camp. Asked to fight orcs she rates as beneath Ollendor, she refuses until one attacks her anyway, then she dumps him and flies up.

Need and help pull two different faces. Theatrical clinging and innuendo are cover, especially around Kate. Fairness after someone actually hosted or helped her is the sincere variant: she will not smear him, and she may concede “only a little.” Kindness after a secret drops can look like mind-control to her—“what did you do to them?”—before she risks a little trust.

Kate’s distrust gets mockery, extra oaths, and (when a ruse needs genuine shock) a staged betrayal. After Kate’s later apology she uses Kate’s name once, then “icey” again. Years later, Kate saying she would never knife a friend produces relief large enough that Melindra admits she had expected a dagger.

Combat hiding is the default until capture, dissection, or a friend’s direct ask. She will not blast into a golem melee (friendly fire), will not fly under certain aerial-priority drones, and will veto control-room heroics while understrength. A boulder rain gets a wind barrier she already knows is weak against rock. Position compromised means extract now, not another errand. Pride yields immediately to a threat that can end them in seconds; she kneels, stays as calm as she can, and still corrects facts.

Call her clothes sleepwear and she lectures in earnest. Kate asking her not to tell Barry a plan is a reason to tell him: friends may inconvenience each other. Goblins touching her rocks get a smile plus a wind-blade threat, then the stone back once it has been used. Oath pressure after the king is dead looks like fidgeting and trouble breathing; to Daren she says nothing is wrong. Dead air in ruins becomes romance poking. Goodbye is short. Flower calling right after takeoff: protest, then she answers. An incoming fight: she drops the call so she will not distract them.

## Relationships

**Barry.** Fellow half-lessathi, the easy name, and (with Flower) the person she trusts most. She pesters until he likes her, uses him as the one who already knows the control problem, and recasts flight on him often before he finishes asking. Optional intimacy stays framed as no attachments. She will spy on him, coach him through Flower, torment him with rocks, and tease defective survival instincts. She may leave him a luck-charm stone she wants back.

**Kate.** Least likely ally in Melindra’s head at first: ice-mage stereotypes, oaths, cling-theater, “traitor.” Kate attacking on Melindra’s behalf and then apologizing is the shock that lets a little trust in. After that Melindra trains her sharpness, covers her runs, partners on not splitting and on air platforms, and still calls her icey. She wants the later check-ins.

**Flower.** The only easy friendship from the start. Shared rock collection, breakfast runs, orders into a fire that Melindra actually follows. Flower is who she warns off before going all-out. She will not let Flower thank her (first time Barry sees her flustered). At goodbye Flower is the one she will miss most; open-call policy.

**Illuna.** Teasing and tactical overlap, not the rock-hobby warmth. Melindra hides a follow, needles three-serpent overkill, and treats Illuna’s aura discipline as the concealment model. Illuna is who talks her down from strangling Eiden over a possible exposure.

**Daren.** Law, oaths, and limiter fear. She underestimates him until she hears his tactics, then still finds a “stay out of ordinary fights” plan a little fishy. She blocks his martyr errands; he blocks her casual kills. She teases him about solo rushes and hugs, panics at oath travel, and thanks him for slow-flight caution when she leaves.

**Hadrik.** Not a friend at first; he is the one trying to integrate her. Later: night cards, easier fit, and shared trouble with the local-protection geas. She nags his aura concealment until the whole layer is gone.

**Arraka.** Known for centuries. Melindra’s first act on meeting the party is silencing her. Cold when Arraka airs fox gossip. Spectator at Arraka’s class, not a pupil. Arraka still ranks her as the group’s only real stillwater.

**Eiden.** Oldest living friend: prison-cleaner trades, not kin, not lovers. He set stillwater secrecy, sided in ways she promised not to detail, and has lately ignored her (including a non-hello that rankles). She assumes he will not attack the party while she is in it. She will not help murder him for a Magium connector. His exits without goodbye reopen the grudge.

**Leila.** Practical: extra stealth makes extra passengers fine; aura answers when Leila asks who was a mage. Notices a post-kiss blush and stays quiet.

**Rose.** Genuinely impressed by a conscious teleport; explains why a world-freeze will not obey her.

**Brother (supporting).** Offstage reason she wanted what the king of Ollendor stole. He fell in with people who fooled him; she has leads and can travel faster alone. She does not confirm other people’s guesses about hostages.

## History

Born in Varathia, half-lessathi. The stillwater change broke ordinary stat-device recognition; she stopped aging, is sterile and mostly disease-immune, not immortal. She met Eiden as a prison cleaner, traded better food for his cell rocks, and stayed friends under a long silence about the still winter. She trailed him and the golden fox then; the fox still hates her. She has not met another stillwater besides him in ages. A past travelling group tried a limiter on her in her sleep.

She entered the tournament to reach Ollendor. The king had exiled her (about eight years before the party meets her) and taken something she needed. Tournament geas: she cannot hurt Varathian locals. She joins the party under a blood oath: no harm to them, then lead them onward after the king is dead; optional extra protection from her “friend.” She sits out ordinary fights and hides a control problem until a sage fight forces both the ruse and the blades into the open. After that she is more normal with the group—some night shifts, less ill intent in the old provocation habit—while still refusing suicide training and still treating exile and the stolen object as personal.

She pathfinds Ollendor (and can still get lost after a remodel), trains Kate, and recovers a ring from the dead king. Oath anxiety then outranks city politics except where friends will still live there. She delays naming the pinecone contact until she is forced to, then flies off to schedule the meeting. She refuses a Magium deal that would target Eiden, leaves on her own currents to look for her brother, and keeps a transceiver open—especially for Flower, and in secret for Kate.
