# Kate

## Overview

Kate is an ice elementalist and a core companion across all three books. She is an artificial mage: not born to magic, and never intending to become one. She returned to Varathia through the tournament to find a friend, not to claim the Magium. Guarded, debt-keeping, and lethal when she judges someone a future hunter, she rations trust until proof lands, then spends herself to keep the people she has already claimed.

## Appearance

Slim and tall, early twenties, with long black hair, fair skin, and a serious face. She dresses in leather pants, a long-sleeved white blouse, and a green hooded cloak, which she has used to pass as a man in a mage crowd. Her default look is a cold stare; she hides feeling on her face and often looks exhausted after strain, masking it as fatigue. Barry reads her features as less soft than Rose’s, describing them as feral only on the route where a light scar remains on the left side of her face. At eleven, in Beacon memory, she is a smaller version of herself in rags, a “reject” without a collar.

Her magic is ice: shields, spheres, cones, daggers, fans, platforms, walls, beams, cages, storm-proof domes, flying ice cages, and, at full commitment, an ice dragon that looks almost alive. She prefers high icicles for range, power, and control; freezing spells she cannot undo. She fidgets by conjuring and tossing an ice dagger. She also trained in martial arts without magic, can throw a trained fighter, and later learns aura suppression and aura interruption.

## Personality and tendencies

Information is rationed. She is curt, watches her back, and answers personal history with delay, “it’s complicated,” or a walk-away rather than a cover story. Fear and guilt come out as coldness, snark, or calling someone an idiot, not as distance she actually keeps. She keeps a running debt ledger and collects or pays it promptly: extra night watches for “trouble” she caused, ice for a rescue she thinks she owes.

Survival pragmatism sits over white-mage mercy. She will not pick a fight she cannot win, will not murder civilians or guards for convenience, and will not pretend an ally is trustworthy because it is convenient. When she judges an enemy a future hunt, she finishes them. Combat is overwhelming ice, often from cover, with group formation protected before her own offense. Collared or banned from magic, she fights with a knife until encircled, then breaks the ban rather than die. Once committed, she ignores even Daren’s caution and will faint if full power costs that.

She isolates herself to protect others: private confrontations, claiming she came alone, offering to enter a lair without the group. She rejects pity and the banshee label; indifference to that word from someone she likes can make her laugh. For Diane she will plead, duel, and refuse retreat. Grief shows as denial, then withdrawal and “this isn’t fair,” not as a speech. She tries to save everyone, including betrayers, longer than the situation rewards. Structural analysis (politics, Magium, lessathi motives) is how she thinks when she is not fighting. She is not competing for the Magium.

Trust moves on proven help, aura reads she believes, and shared combat, not on words. Melindra is the late exception to “do not ask friends for inconvenient help.” She dislikes being read even while she reads Barry through his act.

## Reactions and behavior

The banshee label hurts first (tears, a downcast twitch, looking at the ground when strangers scream it), then turns into combat fury. Being called defenseless, a whore, a monster, or “young lady” produces immediate indignation. Stillwaters start as mass murderers in her moral map; she does not drop that after one battle.

Exhaustion and injury get “I’m alright” and “I’ve been in worse.” Asked how she is, she looks aside, downplays, then may admit sleep trouble, and hide less when it is only one person. After a trial or ritual she puts a fatigue mask on and asks who is left and what can still be sabotaged. She refuses to be used as a spell dummy; Beacon experiments are why.

Romance rumor gets a tense private question and a clear not-interested, or relief if denied. Ice to the throat if someone jokes that she confessed. Asked if she has feelings, she snaps “Have you lost your mind?” then gives a careful, delayed answer: friendship as the reason she looks out for Barry, the rest punted until after survival. A hypothetical duel with him: she would not kill him, and she wants to know if he would. Dawn toasts she always passes.

Pity gets “Do I look like I need your pity?”, then a walk-back that she did not know what to expect. Offered friendship after she tried to kill someone, she calls them insane, then shakes on it. Failed kill or being saved: apology, a cling, and “why did you save me?” Beaten after refusing surrender, she goes silent like someone awaiting execution. Surprise competence from Barry gets an honest “I didn’t expect you to pull it off.” Tactlessness after she tried to be kind gets a smack or a hair-yank, not a lecture.

She converts Beacon trauma into procedure: fake kindness was a loyalty test, so she gives a panicking captive an order instead of comfort. Enemy honor talk in a fight is answered with ice through the opening. Incomplete intel delays a political decision; feeling she ruined an alliance sends her off without asking the others. Incomplete healing or a new scar becomes a dry joke that people already called her a monster.

Protective panic is loud and specific: Barry mutilated, Flower crushed, Eiden choking Rose’s remains or Diane. Guilt that she cannot name (a kill she did not want known) is mute, sweating, ground-staring until someone says her name. When she has already decided someone is a future threat, spare-them logic that is empty does not stop her.

## Relationships

**Barry.** Distrusted stillwater, then useful specialist, then a named ally on a debt ledger. She trusts that he does not look at her as a monster, not that he tells the truth, and she scolds both his lies and his mouth. After accusing him of being lessathi and failing to finish the kill, she believes he has been keeping her alive since they met; friendship is restored on his terms, while she still expects to be a danger to him. She looks after him as a friend, polices promises when Rose is in the picture, and will not compete with him to the death. Worry is framed as annoyance. She later talks him through Magium hesitation, may teach him ice, and values the friendship without letting other people’s romantic reading of her stand unchallenged.

**Daren.** Chosen escort and white-mage threat in one. She trusts his legend immediately, fears purification, and will not list her weaknesses to him. She uses him as healer and tank, reads whether he looks at her as human, and treats an eventual fight as something he wanted from the start. Snark, then healing accepted without warmth. She yields when he is firm, follows his call to dump offense into a dome, and will still ice-wall him if he moves to kill what is left of Rose.

**Rose.** The first person besides Barry who does not treat her as other. Gratitude after Rose body-blocks an attack is unusually open; Kate feels responsible for her and warns her about being seen with wanted outsiders. If Rose dies, Kate is the worst-hit walker: denial, revenant hope, then ground-staring silence. If a revenant remains, Kate will not call it Rose and will not let Daren execute the scrap that is left. She protects a living Rose from overusing time-freeze.

**Diane.** Best friend and the reason Kate returned to the continent. Kate cannot process Diane choosing lessathi collaboration, refuses to leave her, and wants to “beat some sense” into her. After they reunite, Diane is hard to read; there is no clean ending of simply taking her away. Wrestling restores a Beacon-era ease Kate had not shown since the reunion. Secret training misses Kate calls embarrassing.

**Leila.** A Beacon inmate Kate believed dead because lessathi said so; they were not close then because Leila was lessathi. Reunion is apology-first, then shopping, trail talk, and fight cover. Kate is protective to the point others have to pull her focus back to the battlefield. Later she finds Leila easier to connect with than Diane, despite less shared Beacon history, and names her among the people she and Barry get along with best.

**Hadrik.** A stranger who blocked a mob for her; she is shocked he would stake a Diane lead on Barry, thanks him, and tries to pay. She brakes his threats against locals, objects when he “bans” himself to save her, then accepts the extra round. She trusts him to occupy a heavy fighter so others are not knocked out.

**Flower.** Kinship as ethereal and artificial mage: Beacon talk, survival habits, an invitation to stay. Soft check-ins that she drops if Flower shouts. Flower copies Kate’s cold, efficient speech; Kate is embarrassed when that is said aloud. She is shocked if nobody mentioned Flower’s birthday and will tell Illuna to ease up on Flower’s evolved form.

**Illuna.** Teacher of aura suppression; student of massacre history that Kate then lets stand. Past small-talk distance after a humble correction. Kate panics if Illuna is stabbed, coordinates freeze with Illuna’s serpents, and accepts Illuna physically blocking a solo mission.

**Melindra.** Open hostility from the first forest hour: stillwater, Eiden’s friend, information leak, “the enemy” if a couple-act lands. Kate wants her oath-bound before strategy is spoken. Hearing that Melindra would be dissected alive reclassifies her as someone to protect; Kate apologizes for misreading her. Training partner afterward (“icey”); Kate will call her a friend when Melindra expects a knife, and keeps secret training contact after Melindra leaves.

**Eiden.** Suspicious from the first clap. Rescue and assault are the same capriciousness. Far from friends; he needs no logical reason. No trust, and she still will not let him execute Diane.

**Arraka.** Demands usable knowledge (primordial potential, Magium as a bridge, not vibes). Interrupts tantrums, practices aura interruption without asking for lessons, and de-escalates around her when the group cannot afford the fight.

**Meridith.** Beacon authority Kate stood up to as a child. Trial memory plus Diane’s free choice outweigh Beacon hatred for one meeting. She is not hated and not a life Kate wants. Relics she first refuses as sacrilege, then uses.

**Nolderan.** Assumed assassin or banshee-hunter master until shared battle; then an explicit apology for suspecting him.

**Azarius.** Confused her by classing her as a woman mage, not a banshee.

**Enrique.** Distrusted as a guardian until a break-up vow looks genuine; she will challenge him dumping a lover to move in with children.

**Ella.** Kate translates slave logic the others lack and tells them to stop pampering panic.

**Underground king / Breynan.** Distrust from the first meeting, read clearly enough to predict her. Open contempt on succession, surveillance, slavery, and prisoner triage. Alliance is a tool.

## History

Born on Varathia, never met her parents. Abandoned to the Beacon of Hope; lessathi experiments made her a mage. Dumped in a jungle at twelve as a reject. She left the continent and came back through the tournament for Diane.

She approaches Daren for an escort, objects to travelling with a stillwater, and stays because he will not split the party. Early fights establish her as someone who overwhelms hunters and executes captives she thinks will hunt again. She wakes Barry in secret for obscure magic and a private question: why Daren did not purify her. Electric residual magic is treated as a Diane lead and produces her first slight smile.

After the stat-device talk she ambushes Barry, believing he is lessathi and has Diane. It is the first time she tells anyone the Beacon story. She fails to finish the kill, takes a handshake of friendship, then bargains and duels to reach Diane. Diane rejects rescue; Kate will not leave.

Collared in lessathi custody, she identifies the hardware, bonds with Flower over ethereal survival, learns to pass as human at a glance, and asks to stay with the group after the chalice, still intending to find Diane. She mourns Rose if Rose is lost, or hugs her goodbye if she lives.

She denies Leila should be alive, then weeps through a reunion apology for believing the death report and chasing Diane instead. In the arena she leads a weak division, breaks the magic ban rather than die, and becomes an undesirable; she still spends her reserves on Tyrath. A Beacon trial loops her through trying to save Diane, Jason, Leila, and the other rejects until the win condition is not intervening. Afterward she hides the worst of it and immediately asks who can still be sabotaged.

She is not after the Magium. She trains ice to a stillwater-adjacent edge with Melindra, talks Barry through Magium hesitation after the Time ritual, and keeps a hard line on prisoners. Offered a lessathi meeting, she gives Meridith the benefit of the doubt because of Diane. Finding Diane removes the clean “take her away from Varathia” ending. When Eiden says Diane must die, Kate steps in front with the scepter, interrupts his technique, and drains herself on a freeze beam, still raising icicles afterward. A post-fight wrestle with Diane is the first easy warmth since they reunited.
