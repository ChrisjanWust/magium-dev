# Barry

## Overview

Barry is the first-person protagonist: a twenty-eight-year-old ordinary-looking man who joined the Varathia tournament because he believes the Magium can turn a human into a mage. He has wanted that since he was five. He is not a stillwater, though crowds and enemies often assume he is; he is later identified as half-lessathi, with an aura weaker than a typical human’s. He fights with a fortune-teller “stat booster,” a notebook of lore and theory, a dagger, and a crossbow, treating magic as a problem of loopholes and preparation rather than glory.

Player choices color how blunt, lethal, or romantic he is. The durable through-line is the same: he inserts himself, jokes while shaken, protects the people in front of him, and does not quit the Magium.

## Appearance

He presents as an unremarkable adult human. His magical aura is unusually faint; it “shakes” when he lies, which people who can read auras notice. Half-lessathi traits show up as resistances more than as looks: partial paralysis resistance, above-average electricity resistance, and enough sleep-spell resistance that a knockout attempt can leave him spinning on the ground instead of unconscious. After a botched teleport he is nauseated, dizzy, and headache-struck until someone settles the aura around his head.

He carries the stat booster (which he can lock so others see only the screen), an enchanted notebook, and mundane weapons. When magic is unavailable he still fights. Physical boosts amplify fitness he already trained for months before the tournament; they do not invent a different body. In a forced childhood regression he is thirteen again—shorter arms and hands, no gear—inside the isolated cabin where he grew up.

Later he can exist as a bodiless consciousness on the ethereal plane, will a body into being, and briefly occupy an “empty cube” of mind. Once Magical Power and Magical Knowledge unlock, he draws from the magical plane like other mages. First flight on the material plane is clumsy air currents; with more access those currents can stabilize.

## Personality and tendencies

He treats the tournament as tactics and stats, not as a legend about himself. Notebooks, overheard guards, creature weaknesses, and other people’s lectures are the real weapons; he will leave a fight’s stands to fetch a page. He invented a multi-step dispelling method as a non-caster and let someone else take the name. He downplays that kind of history until someone forces the topic.

Default voice is sarcastic and tactless even under annihilation. He undercuts sincerity with a joke, a half-truth, or a stall. He is a capable opportunistic bluffer in the opening stillwater act, and a poor one when he is already panicking; allies call the latter out. He will lie to keep an alliance or a secret he was asked to keep, then sweat and stall when the lie collapses, and finally dump the blunt truth.

He protects first and lectures himself afterward. He puts keeping Kate in the group, pulling a drowning teammate ashore, or standing between a companion and a lethal “purification” above his own safety, then mocks the knife, the duel, or the debt once the danger passes. Loyalty arrives after a survival calculus: he will trade, bargain, or stay silent until someone is actually dying, abandoned, or about to be kicked aside.

Curiosity is a liability he does not retire. He pokes stillwaters, gods, Magium nature, and forbidden origins even when it costs trust, and he bets powerful people will not kill him for being right. Reckless self-insertion is the other constant: rushing a healer, accepting a giant’s spar, offering himself as bait, grabbing a red warning, scouting with a dagger. He knows the streak well enough to ask friends to physically interrupt his worst ideas, then offers a reckless plan in the same speech. After recovered childhood memories, a leftover survival instinct starts to argue with the old “impossible is fine” stance; he covers the shake with jokes and fake bravado.

Killing is a wide player spectrum, from nonlethal at personal cost to leaving no survivors. “Hide and hope they leave” is the option that gets him killed. Combat thinking is trap-first: do not smash the obvious target; look for the real caster, the rebound, the wording of the bet. He prefers planned openings and baited behaviors, then improvises when the plan dies. Later he sets teammates up rather than trying to win a duel alone.

Dying does not scare him the way ceasing to exist does. Magium-linked erasure produces shivering, cold sweat, and frantic analysis. Under prolonged pain he tries to “become one with” it rather than break.

## Reactions and behavior

A mortal bluff that works leaves his heart racing and a self-rebuke for playing hero; he does not undo the act. An overwhelming stillwater or god-scale threat produces freeze and shake, then a bargain, a message, a kneel to protect the group, or an insult—not a permanent lockup. When a god rips that refusal apart, the reversed memory hardens into a vow rather than caution.

A banshee-like aura triggers old panic, then a reasoned distinction if the person in front of him is not his sister. Fresh corpses and first kills range from too busy surviving, to pity he will not let in, to near-vomiting; later lethality toward a surrendered enemy can be protest-then-acceptance or agreement, depending on what he already chose.

When a companion is isolated, drowning, tortured, or about to be purified, he does not walk away. “Don’t help” is wait-and-see, not abandonment. He will draw a blade because refusal is death, take offered power because a victim is in front of him, or stay silent until someone else moves—the last is a real variant, not a rewrite of the first. After he acts, he undercuts the gravity (a boot joke, spinning an amulet, a three-finger gag).

Caught lying, he stalls, then confesses. Caught in innuendo, he sweats and pinches and insists it was only sleeping. When someone he cares about storms off, he apologizes once; if they shut him down, he does not chase. Silence between friends is a problem he solves by volunteering to be the idiot so they have a shared topic.

Official pressure and lectures get dry mockery, interruptions, and the forbidden question again. Honor-duel culture gets a refusal: he fights on his own terms. A crowd booing a spare can pull a lecture about mindless sheep. Alliance “help” from Eiden he reads as a test or a toy, not a partnership.

In infiltration and street fights he prefers buying time, stirring a feud, or coaching mages through traps he cannot cast. Aura work gives him away; he still tries concealment, stone-reading, and techniques he learned the night before. On the ethereal plane he overthinks breathing until theory saves him, then treats the first real spell as earned study, not a device gift.

## Relationships

**Daren.** The fairy-tale healer Barry once admired from afar, and the man he impulsively saved, then leaned on. Barry withholds the Magium motive until forced, uses Daren’s promises and transceiver against Daren’s worst purification impulse, and will condition a sword when Daren is not thinking straight. Publicly he follows Daren’s lead; privately they bicker theory versus practice. Daren treats him as someone who needs protecting and later as someone who has been holding out. Night watches are where hesitation and Magium optimism both show.

**Kate.** He refused to let her be “purified,” hid that the judgment was his, and kept treating her as a person rather than a banshee. She files him as an idiot, yanks his hair, and still uses his obscure-magic knowledge. Neither intends to kill the other if the tournament pits them against each other; Kate instead expects Barry and Daren to fight over the Magium eventually. After the time-god rituals she names the change in him and, with a Magium-plus-device idea, restores his will to win; he credits her for that. She may hide less from him alone. He teases her trust issues; she tries to keep him off the field without stats and later worries over headaches. He can refuse to let her walk into a lessathi lair alone.

**Rose.** First impression is looks; later he talks her through guilt and matchmaking with deflection. If he saves a local girl she is tied to, she may sleep with him as thanks, not a marriage; he can accept or walk it back without cruelty. If she lives, he relies on her as a guide and calls her in crises, including time-freeze, then apologizes when he pushes her too far. A romance-plus-later-entanglement variant can force him to drop a “no commitments” line she asks for. If she dies, he avoids thinking about it until a revenant using her channel hits him like a dagger; he can keep or close that channel, and can defend the revenant from Daren.

**Hadrik.** A sought-after sparring partner who becomes a traveling companion. Barry will not report that Hadrik is a giant, because Barry is equally out of place. They trade drinks, cards, ale, and name jokes. Barry imagines Hadrik would speechify after a win while he just wants rest. He later uses Hadrik as a moving spell dummy and apologizes when he did not know about a hangover. He asks Hadrik, among others, to smack him for dumb plans.

**Flower.** He can mock her age or doubt her and still intervene when someone threatens the shared body. She cheers him, explores with him, teaches him on the ethereal plane, and panics when he fades. He avoids hurting her, stalls on adult/child talk until someone else opens it, and later treats her as guide, last-second rescuer, and rock-talk companion.

**Illuna.** He seeks her out and will claim friendship; she rejects the label and later treats him as a student who already has the theory. He is comfortable needling her in a way he is not with Flower, and uses a volunteered night watch to reopen their fight. He contrasts her with his sister: Claire enjoyed the killings and could not be reasoned with.

**Arraka.** Informant, insult engine, and eventual teacher. He bargains for warnings, holds or spins the amulet, and threatens it for Magium answers. He endures her mockery, follows drills he calls nonsensical because the theory is sound, and asks how to kill a god. She names him a primordial-magic candidate and a “trump card” while still insulting him; he needles her in return.

**Eiden.** Fear mixed with defiant bargaining. Barry becomes the stillwater’s messenger (“Remember the still winter”), a powered-up test subject, and later someone who names Eiden’s cheats and planned destruction as a tantrum. He refuses to read staged help as alliance, vows repayment for dungeon taunts, and can frame Eiden in private as helper, untrusted, or neither. An accidental leak that Eiden can sense lessathi from afar helps turn the relationship into a real kill attempt. Humor and bluff stay in the toolkit; talking him down at the wrong moment is fatal.

**Leila.** Fellow lessathi gladiator and default partner for planning, fetch-quests, and written conversation that does not bore him. He talks into her silence, remembers she cannot answer a knock, brings clothes and food unasked, and tows her out of water. He engineers her reunion with Kate and promises to keep her safe. He notices her habit of being overlooked. In panic he holds her still and insists they plan together; he will not leave her to crystals or a teleporter alone. A night-shift variant can become a private promise and kiss or a group-only rebuff; a farewell can seize until she forces them to treat it as a short trip.

**Melindra.** Shared half-lessathi spark plus distrust. She latches onto him as an easy name; he stays dry and notebook-first, then trades secrets and undignified tactics (tickling a levitation, holding her wind-control problem until the tornado is about to hit). Optional tent routes range from kicking her out to sex plus an energizer. Her flight spell is both a tool and a calm; he flies her out of her own storm. He covers for her tension around high-ranked lessathi, searches a corpse for what was stolen from her, and can miss her or stay casual at goodbye.

**Diane.** Does not know him when she charges him to keep Kate safe after reading him as lessathi. Later he is suspicious she is Meridith’s plant and still uses her as a willing spell dummy; he suggests asking her about lessathi plans.

**Cutthroat Dave.** An enemy created by the stillwater bluff and Daren’s rescue. Barry may later use Dave’s attack on Eiden as cleanup, intervene to prevent a gruesome death, or fight Dave himself.

**Meridith.** Identifies him as her sister’s half-lessathi son. He accepts a revised deal that keeps the tournament in play, delivers Eiden’s message, and treats Magium access as suddenly realistic—then leaks more than he meant to.

**Supporting.** He bargains with talking deer when he can understand them. He may volunteer bad news to children or let Daren do it. He distrusts arena division-mates as teammates while still using them as tools. Albert is mutual dislike. He can greet, ignore, or mock escort duty. He may promise a loud rescue Daren calls reckless. He is wary of being Eiden’s spectacle and will mimic stillwater casualness to intimidate a healer.

## History

Raised on an isolated farm after his birth parents were murdered, Barry was adopted by Helen and Jonathan. At five he fixed on becoming a mage. About fifteen years before the tournament, his sister Claire awakened as a banshee, killed their parents, and he impaled her with a pitchfork to stop her from killing his younger brother. For years he told himself he had arrived too late; a later ritual forces the true memory—he was there, froze, and only struck when the brother was about to die. That freeze is the root of the defective survival instinct others name in him.

From seventeen he drifted the Western Continent through libraries and odd jobs, writing spell theory he could not cast, training his body, and buying a stat booster shops were already cagey about restocking. He entered Varathia as an ordinary human among mages, bluffed a stillwater reputation, and attached himself to Daren and then Kate.

He trades with a stillwater for the party’s lives, picks up Rose (whether or not he ran toward a scream), Hadrik, and later Flower, Illuna, and Arraka. Collars, a city stay, and an arena sentence separate him into his own division, where he meets Leila and keeps fighting by notes, sabotage, and loopholes—including a forced duel he survives by following Daren’s surrender. Melindra joins on the road. He argues with Eiden over cities, keeps other people’s secrets badly, and is dragged through a god’s trial that restores the cabin and the pitchfork.

Afterward he is privately shaken; Kate’s workaround for Magium-plus-device restores the will to win. Underground Ollendor, rebels, an Overseer, and a botched teleport put him and Leila among Magium-linked crystals. He learns he is Meridith’s nephew, that his biological parents were killed after they fled, and that ethereal-plane study can become real casting. He delivers the still-winter message, treats becoming a mage as the prize that finally looks reachable, and walks into a fight with Eiden using practiced setups rather than raw power.

What he withholds, whom he sleeps with, whom he saves, and how many people he is willing to kill are route-dependent. That he came to win, and that he will not abandon the people already standing next to him, is not.
