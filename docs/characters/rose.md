# Rose

## Overview

Rose is a Varathian herbalist in her mid-twenties: a local volunteer, older sister, and traveling companion whose first instinct is to feed, explain, and make peace. She is not a mage. What later gets called time weaving arrives by accident, under panic, and is aimed at undoing harm to people she loves rather than at winning fights.

She stays with the group as a guide and social glue, then—if she lives—returns to Thilias and supports them from a distance while raising her siblings. A later path can remove her from the story entirely. That outcome is not her character; the profile below is the living woman.

## Appearance

Long auburn hair, a thin waist, soft features, a warm smile, and light-brown eyes. She wears an auburn dress and looks like what she is: a town herbalist, not a fighter. She carries remedies (including leaves that clear drunkenness) and knows local plants well enough to name substitutes when a book does not.

## Personality and tendencies

Default warmth. She downplays her own stories as boring, explains local politics and ecology patiently, and treats courtesy as a tool that works on strangers, retainers, and people the rest of the group has already written off. Labels bounce off her; she follows people, not categories.

She is a peacemaker who will still put herself in the path of a killing blow. Trust comes first, then social repair: she softens others with herbs, jokes, and voting blocs rather than with force. She believes the best of helpers until someone gives her a concrete counter-example.

Caretaking is the through-line—herbs, hospital rounds, siblings, guests, hangover cures, a last-night feast. She translates outsider morals into local survival (do not attack a noble in the street; do not say “free” where that word means a death sentence) and is capable of looking away because looking is harder. When she later names that habit, she does not argue the accusation.

When she decides, she decides: a locked door, a job offered, a hospital route grabbed to kill gossip. Power, when it comes, is unplanned and exhausting. She is honest about its limits and will still push past them once the group is committed. Siblings come before the party’s conversation. She worries about other people more than about herself.

## Reactions and behavior

Rescue and thanks get an elegant bow, a polite smile, and practical talk. Starving attackers get food and a rule—“don’t attack defenseless people”—not punishment. Friends arguing over whether to escort her: she asks them not to fight on her account and claims she knows the safe roads.

Kate under a sword: she steps in, citing a life-debt and her standing as a local. Kate and Barry bickering: a giggle, and a comparison to her little siblings. Sudden flirtation: a chuckle, a delay, or a clean decline. Confirmed mutual liking: decisive, playful, in control, and not a claim on anyone’s future. A recanted pass: no sulk, still thanks.

Guilt drops the smile. A missing party produces panic, then “I’ll be right there.” Reunion is relief first, questions second. Sacred woods get a cut lecture and a move. Dragon-scale monsters freeze her; forest-spirit caution is practical, not theatrical. She blushes and throws a light punch when teased, and she is apologetic when she outvotes someone.

Street talk that could get them killed earns an anxious hush, not agreement. A public cruelty she failed to stop leaves her staring at the ground, then doing the next useful thing anyway. Friends in mortal danger: she pleads, then the time gift fires from wanting it undone, not from technique. A premonition arrives as urgent, specific instructions. Asked to hold the world still, she fears losing control and does it anyway. After a fight, the first question is whether anyone died.

## Relationships

**Barry.** Grateful from the first rescue. She treats early passes as premature, not cruel. If a night happens later, she frames it as thanks with an exit, not a claim, and she will flatten disappointment into “I understand” rather than make him quit what he came to do. He is her usual caller in a crisis.

**Kate.** Defended as a person before they are friends; Rose draws out rare smiles, asks if she needs rest, and treats staying with her as obvious. From the stands or a transceiver, Kate is someone Rose will spend an accidental freeze to save.

**Daren.** Escort, pack-enchanter, and the protector she thanks and worries for—not her lover, however much a host may joke. She stops him from a street blow that would wreck them, still uses him as a healer, and jokes about his “usual optimism.”

**Flower and Illuna.** Immediate warmth for Flower; she wants her to stay and treats the childlike manner as a surface over someone kind and serious. Illuna is the one Rose somewhat trusts, and the first premonition she reports is aimed at keeping Illuna alive. She introduces them as two people in one body.

**Hadrik.** Friendly on first meeting: handshake, questions about why he fights, later laughter at a send-off tease.

**Eiden.** She introduces herself, asks if he is a friend, and defends him as a savior until a concrete “on a whim” framing deflates her. His later accusation about looking away lands; she does not argue it.

**Enrique, Suzie, and Kevin.** An amicable ex-husband who left her a house she still calls modest because she did not buy it; she giggles at the memory of a charming serial lover rather than raging. She trusts him with Suzie (8) and Kevin (9). Soft with Suzie; she will leave a call the instant the child needs her.

**Elaria and Molan.** Old herb-ointment friends among the deer. She withdrew after someone from her town killed Molan’s father in the human–animal war, still cannot meet Elaria’s eyes at first, and apologizes on sight.

**Ella.** If given the chance, she turns a rescued slave into a paid maid and a companion for Suzie—the “selfish request” to stay is how she offers a home.

**Melindra and Arraka.** Melindra is a stranger who still gives usable advice about freezes. Arraka gives training orders; Rose takes them without swagger.

## History

A local herbalist collecting rare plants for an oildrip outbreak the poor could not pay to treat, she was surrounded by goblins, rescued, and then fed and instructed the same goblins. She put herself between Kate and a killing blow, joined the fast road at Daren’s insistence, and kept traveling as guide and companion through forests, ruins, and the approach to Thilias.

Home is her ex-husband’s mansion and a town that will not spare her: siblings, Nurse Martha, the quarantined Sparrow’s district. There she first rewound a massacre she could not bear, without knowing how to repeat it. After that night her path splits. Alive, she hosts, spectates, and later becomes remote support—transceiver, short freezes, uninvited premonitions—while the children stay first. If she does not survive Thilias, later uses of her name and voice are not her.
