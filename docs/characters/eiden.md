# Eiden

## Overview

Eiden is a stillwater who looks like a teenager and has lived for about six centuries. After the still winter he and the golden fox founded Varathia’s later civilization; in Ollendor he is still called Creator. He moves through Barry’s group as a recurring test, bargain, and interruption: he keeps deals, withholds answers for amusement, and treats killing, mercy, and aid as equivalent instruments. Closed eyes, a polite smile, and an aura that can look as weak as a soldier’s or heavy enough to make the air hard to breathe are the surface of a long, slow contempt for humanity that later revelations make sense of without turning him into a simple villain. He is not on anyone’s side. He is watching whether anyone can prove him wrong.

## Appearance

He looks about eighteen: loose sleep-like tunic and pants, hands in his pockets, usually floating. His eyes stay shut in populated places; he closed them long ago at “the ugliness of humanity” and uses magical sense instead, which also defeats illusions. That sense can cover Varathia and pick identity, distance, lies, hesitation, age, race, and mental state from an aura. He cannot read a fully masked aura.

The smile is the tell. It is polite until it widens, goes wicked, or drops. His eyes open only at rare shocks: sorrow at some of Barry’s answers about slaves, anger when Barry’s speech lands as a tantrum, surprise when Daren kneels and asks him to be proven wrong. Unleashed, his aura makes the air heavy and the sky feel ready to crack. Masked, he is invisible until he chooses to speak, then gone without residue. Blue crystals react to him in a way they do not to other stillwaters.

## Personality and tendencies

Boredom and spectacle govern him more than factions, tournaments, or cities. He spectates invisibly until a pact, a demonstration, or a city’s fate is at stake, then stages what others will “discover.” Politics is theater. God-tier conflicts he prefers to settle with contracts and performance rather than a finishing blow. When the show is over he leaves; he does not manage the aftermath.

He frames destruction as mercy, lesson, or side effect, and cruelty as what people already wanted. Gifts are never only for the person who asked. He will honor a bargain he made and still use overwhelming force as the enforcement clause. He withholds explanations because “where would the fun be?” and wins with technicalities—time-freeze loopholes, sleep that overloads wards, a once-a-day electric “paralysis”—then lets the display look like raw power.

The durable thesis is humanity’s indifference, not any one noble, king, or thug. He lectures, then hands out the tool that makes the lecture bloody. He plants continent-wide waiting spells as infrastructure. He treats other people’s crises as timing and data. He would rather remove one unverifiable person than track every leaked sentence. He will still leave a crack for “prove me wrong” after trying to kill the people he is testing.

Late history clarifies the pattern without replacing it. He once believed unforced humans would choose good. Disillusionment was slow: slavery in his cities, a royal betrayal, contempt for “sheep,” the closed eyes, Olmnar, the break with the fox, leaving the continent. The smiling stillwater the party meets is that person, not a separate mask.

## Reactions and behavior

Sarcasm and “blind stillwater” jabs get a polite smile, then a philosophical trap and force. Thugs who try to intimidate him get a wider smile and a massacre he enjoys. Lying to his face is an instant kill. Admitting you used him earns a lesson in pain, then the smile returns. Cartoonish villainy offends his taste; wasted magic earns a lecture. A challenge he accepts with a rule that keeps it interesting. When nothing further entertains him, he teleports out.

He says he only came to help while he escalates. Daren’s idealism is ground into a demonstration; Kate’s “none of our business” is filed as pragmatism; a vow to save every slave can open his eyes in sorrow, then a smile and “I will be looking forward to it.” Told to save them himself, he answers, “Perhaps I will.” A kneeling hate-speech that still asks him to be proven wrong shocks him into granting power anyway. He later warns that the man in that trial is not him.

He mutes people who shout his name, needles ambassadors instead of delivering rescue speeches, and corrects “continent of Varathia” as an island whose people inflated the name. Execution is too dull; an arena is a dare a proud city cannot refuse. Barry’s impertinence is why he keeps visiting. Talk of de-escalation disappoints him as cowardice. Once a fight stops being a demonstration—once he is actually constrained—he drops the performance. If a detection net is held hostage, he will eat weeks of work rather than kill the blackmailer on the spot.

## Relationships

**Barry.** Messenger, amusement, and “great potential.” Eiden greets him as a fellow stillwater, saves him to keep the game going, punishes him for baiting or skipping a delivery, and sets tests of honesty, city-scale change, and whether the new mage will actually fight. He is fascinated and easily angered; an honest aura cools him. Barry’s theory that Eiden wants to be wrong about humans fits the “prove me wrong” refrain better than kindness does.

**Daren.** The armored healer as idealist toy: crushed, slept, paralyzed, needled about never giving up and saving everyone. After a life-for-life trade Eiden will not kill him, but he will not answer a greeting either. The exception is the kneel that asks to be proven wrong. By the end Eiden notices Daren’s shift from blind attacks to hesitation and interrogates that change.

**Kate.** Named as a Beacon survivor without being asked. Her refusals are pragmatism; her interrupt of his choke delights him as underestimation. He still uses people she cares about as levers. Her next meeting with lessathi leadership concerns him too.

**Rose.** First called a time weaver; courtesy on the way out is accepted at face value. She is also a pressure point. A revenant wearing related power is a specimen for Magium revival, not Rose.

**Flower and Illuna.** Twenty-year acquaintance. He greets Illuna by the sacred-woods massacre, not Flower’s name, until they fight. Flower names him the destroyer of Olmnar; her grief does not move him. Flower’s value to him is as the only host Arraka can possess. He keeps the fox’s “do not kill her” request for the time being.

**Arraka.** Old battlefield opponent from the still winter; she “forgets” to brief the party and ruins his invisibility gags. He treats her as a Magium and primordial-magic peer, and is confused that she would spend trainees on gods.

**Hadrik.** Brief, almost courteous sparring interest; no on-stage answer when Hadrik yells for the same power Barry once received. Instinctive strikes are hard for Eiden’s aura-reading to track.

**Melindra.** Oldest living witness who still calls him friend. He taught her an incomplete aura-masking invisibility, then ignored her after she returned to Varathia, including at the Thilias arena. He will not attack the party while she is in it, and she will not help murder him. Gifts and apologies (a Dedra stone) do not include a goodbye; she takes the snubs personally and still will not break her still-winter silence except by accident.

**Tyrath.** Old acquaintance, enemy, and pact-mate. Eiden baits him for sport, treats rematches as reunion theater, and will not let him glass a city in true form. Arraka says he keeps the dragon for amusement; Melindra thinks he would be sad to see Tyrath killed.

**The golden fox.** Former near-inseparable partner in all but species; co-author of the golden-rule civilization. The relationship decayed over slavery in his cities, his faith in unforced goodness, a king and queen’s betrayal, then Olmnar and an official falling-out. Her forest confinement exploits that split; Tyrath does not attack her outside sacred woods because Eiden would teleport in. Eiden still keeps some of her constraints.

**Eleya.** An “old friend” whose tracking he strips in front of her. Olmnar, he says, was not her call.

**Varathia’s rulers.** Ollendor bows to the Creator; he performs respect, frames crises so kings will perform, and punishes broken golden rules—blue crystals are civilizational, not relics—with near-death and “rewards” of honesty that are worse than silence. Ambassadors stay terrified and useful. Golmyck’s populist vandalism is an entertaining spectacle, not an alliance. Diane, as Meridith’s masked second, must die because a promise from her cannot be verified. Meridith he had hoped was more open-minded; her plan he treats as existential, this world and others. Azarius he will hunt once the detection spells are no longer needed.

## History

In the still winter he took the lessathi side, killed most other stillwaters, and fought Arraka. He met a time weaver then; Melindra will not discuss it. With the fox he founded the civilization that still calls him Creator. Varathia predates him; the title does not. For a long stretch he and the fox were nearly inseparable. He believed humans left unforced would choose good. Slavery in his cities, a king and queen who ran dog-eating shows (he fed them to their own dogs), and growing contempt ended that faith. He closed his eyes, destroyed Olmnar as punishment for sport-hunting that started the animal war, fell out with the fox, and left the continent.

He returns around the tournament looking for dark-blue robes and a hawk holding the sun, with the message “Remember the still winter”—meant as something other than a call to war. He tests Daren, bargains with Barry, baits Tyrath, and treats the party as an oddity worth rooting for only while they stay interesting. He collects on deals, including power against a dragon and a public stillwater rumor. He demonstrates Varathia’s indifference with a slave and a protection spell, then gifts the bypass that makes intervention possible. As Creator he stops a dull execution, sentences the group to arenas, and later invokes an old three-way pact rather than let Tyrath’s true form end a city.

He warns against city-saving missions: a calamity that wipes every city will come from his actions, not idle watching. Hidden spells wait across the land. He failed to sabotage certain lessathi rituals. In Ollendor he stages kings, ambassadors, and the Overseer for the party’s benefit, enforces the golden rules, and uses Barry and Leila as instruments. Crystals awaken; Varathia’s cities are no longer the main stake. He hoped Meridith would hear the still-winter message differently. When leaks to her cannot be verified, he tries the simplest stop, then leaves after his detection net is held hostage—still telling Barry and Daren they might yet prove him wrong.
