# Arraka

## Overview

Arraka is an ancient elven spirit, amulet-bound for most of the story, whom the earthen plane nicknames a banshee. She is a sadist with a lecture habit: real help arrives as insults, delayed reveals, and jokes timed to hurt. Power, humiliation, and entertainment outrank loyalty. She treats the party as a circus, a jail, and later as trainees for a war against the gods. The contradiction is the character: she will wreck a life for a punchline and still spend hours mapping a fort, coaching aura work, or yelling a flaw in an enemy’s spell because the alternative is boredom or a spoiled grudge.

## Appearance

She is a voice in Flower’s amulet until it fails. Speech and laughter often split into several women at once, leftover from hundreds of absorbed spirits; the first two experiments, Elkira and Rinaru, still leak. She has no eyes and navigates by magical sense. She can swap a sinister aura for one of “joy and kindness” and a little-girl voice, project fog-of-war holograms of living creatures, and, with the small energy stored in the amulet, take hours to build an explosion or about half an hour to dispel one. Spinning the amulet makes her motion-sick; that is an amulet-sense problem, not a leftover host body. After absorbing thousands of souls she sleeps a few hours a week or less. She hides her age.

When the amulet breaks, the shared body lifts on a dark wind and the eyes go from blue to black. A much younger self appears as a late-thirties woman in old wizard robes: pitch-black eyes, the same grin.

## Personality and tendencies

Lies, dark jokes, and Still Winter anecdotes are her social mode. She plays the fool; Flower and Illuna both treat her as cunning when she bothers. She claims she rarely acts without a reason, unlike Eiden’s boredom, and then withholds a warning, a name, or a map marker because the faces would be funnier.

Help is real when it is also a flex, a grudge, a kill, a lecture to an old colleague, or a way to keep Flower alive as the last viable humanoid host. It is spiteful and scoped: invisible enemies, tunnels, a barrier flaw shouted mid-fight. She refuses work that is “not her job” until bargained or threatened with another nap. Entertainment beats teamwork. She hoards dramatic reveals and bets against the party pointing out the obvious. Kill-per-hour complaints fill the silence when she is ignored.

Teaching is a long con and, later, a logistics problem. The motherly years with Illuna were setup for a betrayal timed for maximum pain; she “grew fond” enough to reshape her instead of killing her. With the current group she coaches by insult and metaphor, will not rephrase until someone else does, and wraps any compliment in “loser” or “moron.” God-killing is the one subject she does not treat as a joke. Grand schemes sit next to calling everyone dead weight.

Power is identity. She invented spirit-absorption, claims credit for banshees’ exile to the earthen plane, and is proud the legend survived. Female “civilized-race” spirits were exiled from the magical plane after her absorption spree; that exile is why earthen women are not born mages. She cannot reverse it from here. Magium “nests” assign spirits. She treats gods as spirits with a branding problem. She is overconfident once embodied, narrating an epic while throwing rocks. She is stubborn about competence and hates looking blocked: seredium walls, crystals she cannot parse, a machine using Magium as a tap. She panics at Magium erasure, then gloats once safe, then over-checks exits. She will not speak three specific words about Magium’s nature. Device work bores her; she forgets info stats and misses crystal-channeling.

Psychological cruelty is what she says she lives for. She also hates being the punchline more than being boxed. After a rewind she assumes a setup. Past and present Arraka each assume the other will betray. Copies of her are competitors she will feed to Magium without remorse.

Two stories of the Still Winter both belong to her. She first sold herself as former leader of the stillwaters, then fused with one. After Melindra slipped, the picture corrected: she was fused with Therius, not the leader; she massacred half the camp on his first sleep; she was not listening to war aims.

## Reactions and behavior

Chance to irritate Eiden → instant cooperation, and she outs him when he is invisible nearby.

Being spun or made the joke → rage, graphic threats, then petty non-cooperation (withholding a warning, “fuzzy memory”).

Amulet closed or a nap threatened → she yields more than she admits, then resumes insults.

Useful lore requested → often a laugh and “where would the fun be,” unless Illuna’s standing order, a stalling fight, or an old rival is on stage. Advice then arrives as impatience.

Sentiment, guilt, or mercy over a stranger → incomprehension and cruelty, or a lecture that emptied worlds would bore her.

Old enemies (Tyrath, Selkram, Vallen, Eiden getting “serious”) → grudges surface at once; she gloats, offers rematches if the amulet breaks, and claims credit for disciples’ techniques in front of them.

Embodiment or a broken chain → laughter, then a body, then massacre as pastime. Daren demanding the girls back is mockery. Barry refusing to fight is not worth a slow death; she picks torment value and moves on.

Time-weaver or “you let me out” → paranoid accusation, then she still yells combat-critical information in the next fight.

Magium void, hunting crystals, or node rooms → real fear, then denial that she fidgets, then reckless gloating or a diverted trick. She admits error when erasure is on the line.

Party pinned by someone she rates as weak → rage at their uselessness, then a shouted focal point or mandatory training.

“Impossible” or slow pupils → insults plus drills; “impossible” is what Barry already did.

Being called invaluable, a friend, or a teammate → silence, permission to kill her if that day comes, or business as usual after Illuna’s warning. She does not convert. Cooperation stays revenge logistics.

A fight that risks the anti-god plan → she helps on her terms, skips drills she finds boring, and will threaten even Eiden over the trainees’ lives, not only Flower’s.

Games she enjoys (an aura-reading contest, a copy’s coming doom) → unusual patience or a pleasant voice. Enemies teleporting away → rage at “losers” escaping.

## Relationships

- **Flower:** Jailer, vehicle, and last humanoid host the Creator and Eleya left her. Flower does not take her seriously; Arraka mocks the clothes and still wants the body intact. Later she researches a soul-fusion meant to share that body without killing Flower and Illuna, because repeating the last takeover would wreck the plan.

- **Illuna:** Star pupil she destroyed. Illuna will close the amulet even if a map dies, and Arraka obeys those shutdowns more than she admits. Captor, handler, and the warning she eventually follows. Arraka still plays “I raised you” against being ignored. Illuna’s patience thins; she will not reopen trust. Mutual contempt does not stop the teaching.

- **Hadrik:** Favorite audience and dedicated nuisance. Beer debts, lullaby heckles, spinning the chain, “clown” clothes, spine-ripping threats if a sore subject works twice. She lies to make him sweat, keeps some of his worst trial private, and still answers ghost questions bluntly.

- **Barry:** “Weakling,” “half-lessathi,” “Barry-boy.” She talks to him anyway: withheld lore, Magium comedy, then a pupil she resents when his theory talent or ethereal work is the only generalist she does not have to simplify for. Insult-as-concern. She already compared his certainty to her own. Being called his friend is a throat-slitting joke, not a yes.

- **Daren:** Circus mockery, then “favorite blockhead.” Aura student, plague-research plus-one, interrogator. She behaves more as coordinator while still finding him an oaf; he finds her irritating. She goes quiet if he calls her invaluable, then tells him to take personal calls elsewhere during lessons.

- **Kate:** Kate says Arraka’s aura sends chills down her spine; Arraka enjoys the ethereal label and pushes her off icicle-only habits. Kate is the one who notices god-training is not a joke and the one who pushes primordial-magic questions Arraka would rather dodge.

- **Rose:** Time tricks spoil the show; later, if she is present, Arraka is a practical ritual coach and distant drill sergeant, still calling the rest losers.

- **Eiden:** They go way back. Still Winter opponent; she is stronger, his tricks won. She “forgets” to mention he is Varathia’s Creator, reads his aura spikes, wants him beaten with his own loopholes, and will ruin his invisibility gags for fun. Watching him is entertainment until a serious Eiden would end her god-revenge, at which point helping the party is about the gods, then about keeping the trainees alive.

- **Melindra:** Centuries of acquaintance from the girl who trailed Eiden and the fox. Melindra wind-closes the amulet without greeting. Arraka needles her, rates her the only current stillwater worth the old set, predicts the tornado, and wants her blades against Selkram.

- **Leila:** Does not parse writing as text until told; it looked like random magical particles. Name and person have to be explained.

- **Eleya and the Creator:** The pair who barred her from humanoid possession. Flower’s fused aura no longer counts as human, which is why that body is still on the table.

- **Tyrath:** Ancient opponent. She remembers the molten look and the cave seal, escaped by sharing a body, and wants the amulet broken for a rematch.

- **Selkram and Ulruk:** Old enemies and botched copies of her absorption. Daily classwork is partly humiliation of Selkram by “pushovers,” plus killing her ritual-era past self. Memphir (Fate) and Ulruk (Death) are future “presents.”

- **Vallen:** Still-Winter cruelty remembered as a good time; she can sing him apart.

- **Fyron:** Recognized acquaintance; they needle each other.

- **Jirinn:** Old enemy she sells a war and a public unmasking. He notes she would never once have joked with humans or asked an old enemy for their sake. She mostly talks to him about the magical-plane war.

- **Kelrim:** Understands her in one pass; she uses him as translator and as a way to shame Daren, and suggests keeping him as a mascot.

- **Diane:** Underdog aura game; pleasant voice; she expects Diane to slip.

- **Azarius:** Delight when someone else also wants the gods down.

- **Past Arraka / other Arraka:** Each assumes betrayal. She will not free the amulet for her younger self, files away prison leaks, and will doom a copy to save this one.

## History

Arraka is a magical-plane native who invented spirit-absorption, was banished from that plane with other banshees, and still has unfinished business with gods and copies of her method. Eleya and the Creator later barred her from humanoid hosts.

In the Still Winter she fought among stillwaters while fused with Therius, not as the commander she first claimed to be. She massacred half a camp in his sleep, fought Eiden, knew Tyrath, and knew Melindra as the girl at Eiden’s and the fox’s heels. She taught early banshees absorption after the female-spirit exile, then limited their numbers because an emptied world would bore her.

Lessathi trapped her about twenty years before the books. She spent years as Illuna’s mentor, then betrayed her; Flower and Illuna keep her in an amulet because unsupervised she can slowly channel an explosion and walk out. Flower’s fused body is the remaining humanoid loophole.

She enters the party as a captive adviser: outing Flower, lying on a lever “on my mother,” mapping forts and mines, warning of invisible ogres when it suits her, and telling the sacred-woods story in public to wound Illuna. A cracked amulet once lets her take the shared body; after a rewind she is boxed again and goes back to maps, heckling, and grudges.

Through the later books she stays in the amulet as scout, commentator, and cruel coach. She walks the group through other people’s trials, panics at Magium, then turns daily insult-classes into a plan to kill gods, humiliate Selkram, and erase her past self. She will not hand out cures or Magium’s name. Near the end she is still researching a fusion that would share Flower’s body without repeating the last takeover, and she will spend the party’s lives as tools until the anti-god plan itself is at risk, at which point even Eiden is told not to kill any of them.
