# Leila

## Overview

Leila is a mute lessathi outcast who joins the party in Varathia and stays a working companion through Ollendor. She writes in bright blue Common that a modified stat booster pulls from her thoughts. Off the field she is small, self-minimizing, and easy to overlook. On it she is fast, literal, and already moving. Loyalty is not a speech: Kate, her adoptive father, and the people she has decided are hers outrank ambition, legality, and her own comfort. She wants to belong and will work for it; she still plans to live in Ollendor with her father when the traveling is done.

## Appearance

She is about twenty (later counted as nineteen), a head shorter than Barry, frail-looking even with tournament stats maxed. Strength and speed scale off the body, so three in each still looks modest. Long silver hair—once black; Beacon experiments bleached it, and it is already silver in childhood memory. Kate sometimes remembers black. Beige blouse, short leggings, wooden sandals, two belt knives; later dual enchanted daggers that specialists rate above ordinary steel against thick hide.

She laughs without sound. Text hangs in the air; she writes large for a crowd or a distance, and embarrassment can blur the letters. She cannot shout, cannot swim, and cannot work a transceiver herself. Writing takes her aura stats offline. In the ether, a voice she built in her head—sampled from other girls and aged—can be heard while her mouth stays closed.

## Personality and tendencies

Quiet until a decision, then fast violence. She does not speechify. Combat is speed, eyes, timing, and the wording of a deal, not giant-skin cutting. She commits once she has a read, including through an illusion if the caster’s attention has slipped. She hates being treated as a damsel and will not play one. Beacon slurs still land; they do not make her helpless.

Off the field she minimizes herself: she will not ask for dry clothes because she did not want to be a bother; oversized borrowed kit gets a pleading look before a meeting. In a group she defaults to a silent corner. Beacon habit plus the delay and placement of text makes joining a conversation feel like interrupting. One-on-one with Barry or Kate she is talkative, curious, and willing to jump topics for a long stretch. She opens up where others perform normalcy; she had already told her ritual while the rest bottled theirs.

Judgment is technical and literal: devices, armor versus mundane fire, a severed troll head that still senses, shopkeepers’ hostile stares before anyone else clocks them, blood on seredium others miss. Aura-reading is a rare skill she will not oversell—it fails often except on easy reads like Barry’s “spiky” lying aura. She stays silent when he is bluffing and explicit when he is not. Terror can blank a target. She uses the same literacy for healing cues, combat assists, and, as Kate has it, lies of omission. Practical checklists come first: call the others, tell Father, get closer together, bribe villagers rather than smash a gate. She prefers a legal survival path to an illegal one that would put her in front of lessathi hunters and artifacts. She still rules-lawyers the arena when killing beasts before the show is safer, and she still notes that safety is not guaranteed off-division.

She wants belonging enough to work hard for a joke membership card, and she is honest about the group’s quirks—Hadrik exhausting, Daren stern—without rejecting them. After a wound she does not insert herself into planning. She will acknowledge care without unpacking it on the spot.

## Reactions and behavior

Confirmation that someone attacked a noble to free a slave gets her first smile, then an introduction. A cell refused is not dropped: she waits, then names him lessathi in public until he agrees. Thugs crowding her get one punch and a kick. Unanswered knocks—she cannot call out—leave her upset and pulling an arm. Water is terror: silent-scream flailing, then clinging. A falling ceiling is mutual: she pulls Barry after he pulled her. If he faints, the text is “please answer me.”

Kate’s name freezes her, then gratitude and an unfinished apology; if the talk is cut short she is distracted until she can write a large public sorry-and-survive. Loyalty to Kate outranks the tournament: she will take Kate’s side even after asking whether Barry would forsake it. Father called trash drops a wait-signal and she stabs. Father missing is the switch that dumps composure—anxiety she tries to talk down, then a rescue sprint. She had not clocked the king or Peter as mages; Overseer hostility makes her ask if they will be attacked.

Charged without an invitation, she is upset and still joins. A lure flagged as risky is still timed. Daren’s decoy critique gets “sorry,” then “I’ll try.” Asked if she is alright after a trial: surprise that anyone noticed, a small nod, “Just a little shaken,” a faint smile, “maybe later.” “Go on without me” gets “don’t be stupid.” Honor-bets and capture-alive orders get a cold stare and an attack on the eyes. A wording trap, once won, is calm: so you will let us go.

Romance and voice embarrass her; she still initiates hard truths. Hearing that someone now reads her writing in the inner voice blushes the text. Fear of uniquely remembering a vanished friend outranks fear of nonexistence; hands tremble when she names it. A two-person future is joy, an impulsive kiss, then a self-conscious recovery so it will not look like regret. A group invitation instead loosens her hand and hides her face behind a forced smile. Farewell dead air is something she names and demands be treated as a normal conversation, not a last rite. Practical first, then the emotional point. Melindra reports that she checks Kate’s feelings before acting on her own.

## Relationships

**Barry:** Fellow stat-wielder from the cells and tunnels; default partner for planning, devices, fetch-work, and signals. She accepts supplies she would not have asked for, thanks him for the Kate meeting, and is surprised when he notices she is used to being overlooked. One-on-one talk with him is easy even on dry subjects. She will not leave him for guards, braces him when he cannot stand, and uses a thought-voice in a Magium storm to make him grab her. Romance is route-dependent. The attachment is not: she is afraid of being the only one who would remember him if he were erased.

**Kate:** Beacon past without a real friendship then—Leila was lessathi and not allowed to socialize much; Kate believed the death report. Reunion is shock, apology, and a rebuilt trust Leila measures as letting Kate talk again. Shared watches, cover fire, shopping, and a willingness to protect one another. Kate finds her easier to connect with than Diane despite less shared history. Leila will make a kingdom an enemy for her.

**Reidzek:** The caretaker who broke her out of the Beacon; she calls him father. He made the writing device. She learned workshop craft and a temple massage from him in secret. She forced him to flee to Ollendor without her, then ran to hug him in a lab coat and introduced “these are my friends.” She loves watching him work and still postpones maintenance when the others sneak out. First thought after a king-threat is locating him. His status with rebels can shelter her; his absence undoes her composure.

**Daren:** Healing, enchanting tools, and a tactics note she accepts without argument. She pins a mage so he is not the only front. In a group he leads she is quiet. He may interrupt a plan of hers and then come back to it.

**Hadrik:** She beats him at aura-bluff cards; he drafts her as referee and scoreboard because she cannot shout names in time. She finds the cheer exhausting but good. Little direct talk later; he is unused to hearing her in the ether instead of reading her.

**Melindra:** A polite sleeve-tug to confirm a regular lessathi aura, then relief. No alliance when Melindra stages a defection; Leila stays. Melindra names her stealth as why extra passengers are fine, answers her mage questions, and stays quiet about a kiss.

**Flower and Illuna:** An ignored handshake at first, then accepted healing and the fused-soul explanation. Shock that Flower is still alive in a banshee’s body, then “I see…”

**Arraka:** Historical fear (still-winter evil), then whether the amulet is safe. Arraka calls her “mute girl” until the name sticks. Leila can tell when Arraka has been shaken.

**Rose:** Brief; Leila trusts Kate’s word that Rose keeps waiting-promises.

**Others:** Bruce’s sudden warrior-respect confuses her; a “fight like a woman” line gets a frown. Wilhelm is knocked out mid-backstab without discussion. Fyron is polite and guarded; she thanks him for a teleport and refuses it. Gontrok is an enemy she tries to kill despite capture-alive orders.

## History

Mute from birth. Her birth father volunteered her to the Beacon; she was a failed experiment. A caretaker—Reidzek, later “father”—broke her out. They hid, then lived under a suggestion-field cabin where she learned devices. At sixteen he gave her the writing booster: a few words at first, then almost all her thoughts. After outcast hunts were banned they moved to a city; he was framed into slavery. Tournament stats let her max speed, strength, and combat technique and free him. She was caught covering his escape.

In the arena she meets Barry, finds lessathi wall-switches, fights cavern beasts, and stays for the legal path rather than an illegal teleport. She reunites with Kate, works the coliseum as a pair with Barry, and leaves with the party toward Ollendor, where she and her father are wanted. She stays when others peel off. A first-stage ritual finishes early; the contents are not shown. Afterward she barely moves.

In Ollendor she spectates, does device work, and is first to force a mage into melee. She hugs Reidzek and writes the introductions. She breaks from father-maintenance to join the party’s next job, asks the question that exposes a king’s vulnerability, and later sprints when Reidzek is taken to work in a seredium tower. A botched teleport drops her with Barry in failsafe caves. She wakes him, will not leave him, fights beside him, and stops a killing blow long enough to ask about her father. After the city’s crisis she helps in the workshop and takes night shifts. When the party leaves, she stays: surface with her father, or a surface meeting if the underground is closed to her.
