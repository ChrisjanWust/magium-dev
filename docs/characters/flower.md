# Flower

## Overview

Flower is an ethereal fire mage who shares one earthen body with Illuna, an awakened banshee she named Petal. They are two souls, two voices, and two wills. Flower lives from the soul’s side of the body; when she fronts, the face is warmer and the eyes are green. Illuna fronts with a colder expression and bright blue eyes. The fusion froze the body’s aging, so Flower still looks about ten while she insists she is turning thirty.

She is circus-trained, ruin-curious, and quick to treat a new group as family. Under the cheer she keeps Arraka boxed in a golden amulet, and she will not stand idle when people—or the idea of them—are being erased. Olmnar, not Illuna’s old massacre name, is the wound that still puts her in a rage.

## Appearance

The shared body is about four feet tall, long red hair to the waist, gray tank top, acrobat’s trapeze clothes from Olmnar, and no shoes. Others call the outfit clown clothes; Flower will not. She will not let anyone braid the hair. She wears Arraka’s golden amulet.

When Flower holds the body, her eyes are green. They go bright red when she readies to fight Eiden, or when she takes an evolved ethereal form: heat radiating off the skin, flames an inch out, long-range soul sense, and a teleport that sickens her if she overuses it. Fire is hers—jets from the feet for flight, fireballs, walls, constructs, drying orbs, cooking. Water, healing, cages, and the daily teleport belong to Illuna. Two auras sit on the same frame, one outworldly and one not; Flower suppresses them toward human-normal.

She is an ethereal by birth. Some aura facts come at a glance without formal study. Spectacle spells click after one look; study-heavy healing does not. Under pressure her magic is instinctive and unstable. In the ether her mind is a decorated house, and she has lived there since she was born.

## Personality and tendencies

Flower is cheerful, literal, and socially clumsy. She panic-laughs when a second person in the body is noticed, blurts unfiltered questions, and treats group acceptance as something unfamiliar. She trusts quickly, volunteers immediately, and would rather explore a hallway than wait in it. Ruins-crawling is a hobby with Petal; hidden passages come before protocol. She is not suited to stealth, and Illuna often keeps her asleep for it.

The childlike register is real: “I just like to blow stuff up,” rock collections, pinecones, waving at strangers, counting birthday candles twice. It sits on top of twenty years of jailer duty. She does not take Arraka’s oaths at face value, she remembers who saved whom, and she will close the amulet for twenty years if Arraka will not be quiet.

She feeds people. Culinary magic, breakfast, arena-memory dishes, cake eaten on the walk so patients are not delayed: hospitality is how she makes a group into a family. A children’s book told her that families have tea and cookies; she has kept that ritual with Illuna for two decades.

She acts against impossible odds when people are being erased, then refuses comfort. Combat jobs get a salute. She credits teammates mid-fight instead of claiming the kill. Strong feeling is her advantage in the ether; analysis is Illuna’s. She can snap into useful seriousness when the group needs it, then drop the performance as soon as Illuna meets her halfway.

She wants to be taken as twenty-nine, then thirty—not as a child, and not quite as an adult. For a while she copies Kate’s cold voice and uses Illuna’s real name as a lever. The act is fear of being a burden, not a change of soul.

## Reactions and behavior

Grief at Olmnar goes to red-eyed fury, then a curt insistence that she is fine. Offered a break, she walks toward the gates. Mocked clothes nearly explode her; a compliment on her hair confuses her. She will not let Daren “save” her from Illuna.

Pressure and shouted instructions make her stiff, mix up left and right, and nervous-laugh. Illuna’s voice or a breathing cue resets her. Horror rooms leave her wordless, then terrified. Age teasing produces a shout, a pout, a performed adulthood, and sad eyes.

She writes events down when the world moves while she sleeps. She remembers traps late and still offers to lead. She cannot abandon the party; unsupervised, Arraka can blast free. Asked about the Magium, she will only say she is not allowed—the oath would kill her and Illuna both.

She panics at side effects she has never seen, opposes a second reckless cast, and still rushes to catch someone who falls. Flat affect she reads as anger until told otherwise. Attachment shows up as frequency: calls, rocks, shared marches, “is everything okay?” rather than speeches. She assumes friendliness until an authority contradicts it. In a last stand she burns her energy too fast.

## Relationships

**Illuna (Petal).** Fused souls; Flower named her before she would give a real name. The happiest day Flower will name is the awakening. Flower fronts exploration, cheer, food, and rescue; Illuna fronts suspicion, water, cages, and banshee politics. They feud over “maturity,” then Flower still needs Petal as an inner coach. Flower thought Illuna was tired of her; hearing that she is fine as she is, and a birthday cake with a no-fight promise, is the reconciliation she wanted. The tea ritual is family she will not drop. Do not credit Illuna’s stasis plays, kills, or Magium confrontations to Flower.

**Arraka.** Jailer for twenty years. Flower values past saves without taking oaths at face value. She wants custody of the amulet, jokes that Arraka will cheer from the stands, snaps her quiet, and once drew a moustache on it. Arraka’s clothing jokes still land as fury. Flower’s fire-cave gullibility, years ago, is how Fyron first met them; she still does not get along with him.

**Melindra.** The fastest, least ironic friendship in the party. Rocks, breakfast, and “because Flower asked” rather than because the group approved. Flower is who Melindra does not feel hated by. When Melindra leaves, Flower is the person who will miss her most, and she calls constantly.

**Barry.** Co-explorer, worry-call, mid-air thanks, puzzle partner, rock peer. She follows his plans without argument more often than she argues them. She is proud when he learns; she is desperate when he fades.

**Daren.** He likes Flower and not the banshee; she notices, and she understands Illuna’s silence. He charged Eiden the same way she wanted to, which softens her. She later coaches him toward ordinary lessathi auras first, then ferries him in a fire wyvern and asks about the fire ward before she lifts him.

**Kate.** First real peer talk: ethereals, Beacon, aura tips. Flower copies her serious manner when she wants to look grown, then drops it. She wanted to show Kate her house in the ether. She shuts down check-ins when she is not ready to be comforted.

**Rose.** Protective, unfiltered, and practical: hungry? tired? She will still offer friendship after a confession that should have ended it.

**Hadrik.** Polite about the amulet, then trust. She stops her own fireballs so he can dive.

**Eiden.** Twenty years of hatred for Olmnar, including the children. She already knows the party cannot beat him and still cannot stand idle. Illuna is the one who physically holds her back.

**Eleya.** Years of unpaid help to the animals did not buy the party a pardon when Flower blocked her. Later the fox still trusts Flower’s word enough to send someone in her care.

## History

Flower is Varathia-born, an Olmnar circus acrobat, ethereal from the start. About twenty years before the party meets her, her soul fused with Illuna’s; the body stopped aging. She named the banshee Petal. They have kept Arraka in the amulet ever since, sleeping in turns so she is never unsupervised.

The party first meets her as a ruins guide who drops from a ceiling hole, slips into “we,” then denies the second person. After she blocks Eleya she is assigned to them as punishment. She consents to Illuna’s two-day stasis for a plan she is not awake to run, wakes after, and chooses to keep traveling. Seeing Eiden, she rages for Olmnar; Illuna holds her.

She stays in the real fight when Illuna is caught elsewhere. She panics for friends she cannot reach, bombs from the stands when she is allowed to act, and will not say what the Magium is. She bonds with Melindra over rocks, insists on saving animals the others would leave, and retries impossible rescues until someone she trusts tells her to let go.

She performs adulthood until a thirtieth-birthday cake and a promise not to fight. In the ether she hosts tea, retrieves lost souls, and takes a form that strains the shared body; Illuna forbids repeating it the same night. When Melindra leaves, Flower calls. She still tries for one pinecone at the border. When Eiden drops her friends, her eyes go red and she fires until he knocks her and Illuna out together.
