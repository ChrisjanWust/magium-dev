# Hadrik

## Overview

Hadrik is a Northern giant traveling as a dwarf. Friends call him “the dwarf.” He joined the Varathia tournament for fights and stories, not the Magium, and stays with the party because the company is better than traveling alone. Fighting is who he is: a clan inheritance he compares to a bird flapping its wings. Humor, ale, and a handshake are his other tools. He keeps deals, defends people being treated as monsters, and will not finish a helpless opponent. Reckless strength is the first move; honor is the last word.

## Appearance

In the form he wears day to day he is about four feet tall, with a black beard to the chest, leather armor, and a jolly booming laugh. As a dwarf he looks about forty; his aura reads as more than a hundred years old. The dwarf body is a shapeshift—short legs he is unused to—and he keeps most of his mass and strength when small. Unarmed punches crater the ground and can return a fire beam.

He can revert to a fifty-foot giant, take a black dragon near Tyrath’s size, become a giant eagle (a weaker body than the dwarf form), or use other shapes for a job: snail-glue, human-size, leprechaun. Years of tavern life among Northern dwarves shaped his accent and manners. He prefers a fighting handicap and often carries spare enchanted tents, dwarven ale, and a shapeshift-scaled backpack that keeps bottles (and sometimes armor) intact.

Aura hardening is a long-practiced trick, not unique to him. He has natural sleep immunity and only partial resistance to paralysis. Some dizziness spells leave him with next-day alcohol nausea. Sober, his style is harder to read; he can counter aura-prediction by not picturing the strike in advance.

## Personality and tendencies

Clan credo: “Mahad selak / Mahad maktar”—glory in life, glory in death. He grins through danger, invents contests, and treats drink as ritual and tradecraft. Drinking, he says, is like breathing; his tolerance is decades long and still below a typical dwarf’s. Teammates who will not toast disappoint him. He makes friends with ale faster than with diplomacy, haggles shopkeepers as sport, and uses loud stories as travel filler.

A deal is a deal: no extra pay, no backing out after a loss. Live-and-let-live applies to other tournament frauds. Chivalry overrides a mob’s consensus; he already had an aura test that banshees and Beacon mages are not the same thing. He will not enjoy executing the helpless, and he walks out on a plan to kill someone while they are weak.

Group travel is a new pleasure. He is the social glue—toasts, cards, pulling wallflowers into a game—and a poor diplomat once he has decided. He notices fractures and tries to patch them with jokes, then with a quieter scheme. He is discreet about other people’s romances and loud about Arraka as a running joke. Abstract cosmology makes his head hurt; he wants a simpler topic: how to hit it, how to not die. Magium talk bores him. He covers fear with jokes, challenges, and the claim that there have not been enough battles. He will not narrate his own Time-God trial.

He does not hold back well even in sparring, and he is too stubborn to take a draw. Reckless brawler first, but he will hand a specialist the fight when the matchup is wrong. When a weakness is named, he trains it that night. Smash-first impatience is followed by an embarrassed apology. He underestimates stealth and paperwork, then explodes at three-day delays.

## Reactions and behavior

Strong names—stillwater, dragon-fighter—draw loud delight, not caution. A woman called a monster puts him in front of the mob: treat a lady with disrespect, then a smirking “Is she, now?” Easy wins disappoint him; a clean loss gets “well fought” and the deal paid. Secret exposed: he laughs, jokes about blackmail, and drops it when no one will report him.

Danger gets a grin and an impatient smash, then a request before the next wall comes down. Empty halls remind him of invisible ogres; he wants Arraka to scan. Waiting behind a barrier makes him complain until someone gives him a job to scare the enemy. Powerful enemies delight him. “Join or die” gets a grin and a refusal. Honor insults get disgust and a storm-out with no looking back.

An innocent killed while friends did nothing feels like personal betrayal; he slams the table, storms off, then apologizes for judging a scene he missed. Squalor gives him the creeps. Idle terror in a rescued girl makes him find her a chore, not a speech. He frames some deaths as honorable and can miss that the point was the people left behind.

He converts grief and revenge into physical comedy: table slams, spinning Arraka’s amulet until she is motion-sick. After she kills them once, that spinning becomes a dedicated appointment; he later stops because she kept his trial private. Crowd silence is a trash-can problem until the chanting starts. He feeds on cheers, argues rules after he has already helped a friend, and uses loopholes and the audience to stay in the fight.

Gontrok’s name darkens his face into a forced grin. The old dwarf-village wound is a live nerve; he trains new size-shifts after two words throw him, will not drop the duel, and still finds a hated enemy’s death and mutilation dishonorable. He slips into present tense about Gontrok afterward and demands to be cheered rather than sit in it.

In council he is blunt and low-prep—“wing it”—then privately sits with old wounds and puts the cheerful face back on when the others return. He wants unanimous votes even for a suicide assault. He is suspicious when enemies are too agreeable. Asked to quit drinking to fight better, he refuses; asked to be a training dummy, he lasts about a minute.

## Relationships

**Barry:** Wanted opponent from the first clearing, then drinking partner, beer-debt accountant, and combat dummy. He credits Barry’s deductions, supplies ale and a punched-open gate when a plan needs it, and will smack him “as a favor” for a dumb idea. He treats Barry’s Magium panic as a missed good challenge and tells him to talk about something else when the flash talk will not stop.

**Daren:** Story contests, needling, and a Magium mismatch—“not compatible”—that does not stop them training as a pair. Barrier-and-punch plans, drinking shifts, and spars that Hadrik will not call a draw. Real rupture only if Rose is dead and Hadrik calls it an honorable choice. Later they share guilt language, golem work, and thought-control in the ether. Daren is the foil who is not always joking, and the one who notices when Hadrik is actually sober.

**Kate:** He steps between her and a mob on principle, then as Diane’s friend. Casual “milady,” an arm-pat after a griffin save, and a bow. He wants her and Melindra to get along and does not adopt Kate’s distrust. He gives her time when she walks out of a meeting.

**Rose:** Easy grin and handshake. Playful about the mansion and Enrique. If she dies staying behind, he names it honor and does not hear the siblings. He introduces himself to Sophie as Daren’s friend.

**Arraka:** The only party member who enjoys her jokes; she helps him because of that. Beer-debt banter turns into dedicated torment after she kills them, then into a debt the other way when she does not leak his Time-God trial. He needles her (“old hag”), celebrates her rare compliments, and still questions her body count. Mock-romance jokes land; he notes “decency” when she keeps a secret.

**Flower:** Easy welcome: fire, walls, and food. He jokes that a “small giant” is the family’s black sheep, lights her birthday candles, and refuses her ale with the rest of the group. On one route, he thanks her for the mind-return that lets him finish a fight.

**Illuna:** He probes the sacred-woods massacre, then drops it when her look darkens. He carries her amplifier and asks for please and thank you, accepting a curt version. He defuses her Arraka outbursts with “we’ll be more careful.”

**Diane:** She beat him in one spell, refused an order to kill him, and taught him how Beacon mages differ from banshees. He stays guarded around her after the sacred woods and ends talks quickly.

**Eiden:** Eager sparring partner under a no-magic rule; loud delight at the stillwater’s lethality. He later yells in the street for the same exception Barry got and gets no answer. He wants a sporting rematch and calls out magic used in a no-magic dare. He will stand in front of Diane when Eiden names her, then grin and charge at full power.

**Melindra:** He does not treat her as a traitor for knowing Eiden. Cards on night watch, nosy teases when she clings to Barry, and a private nod that he respects guts. He wants her and Kate in the same game and does not pick a side in their distrust.

**Leila:** He loses to her at bluff-cards because she reads auras, then drafts her as referee and scorekeeper. She finds his cheer exhausting but good.

**Gontrok:** Old comrades-in-arms, never friends. Shared shapeshifter history and an unresolved dwarf-village crime: war, exile, years raising orphaned dwarf children. Hadrik claims the duel and will not yield it. After Gontrok dies, he refuses retrieval and burial, still finds the end dishonorable, and will not quite admit how hard it landed.

**Others:** Enrique gets a full introduction, banshees included. Ella gets a handshake that scares her and a push toward work, not pity. Nolderan is a drinking buddy until betrayal, then a wall. Kleykarth is an ale-contest friend; Hadrik declines the faction. Meridith’s first pitch is contempt; a revised adventure deal is something he was doing anyway.

## History

A Northern giant of a warrior clan, he spent years in dwarf taverns and locked himself into a dwarf shape for the Varathia tournament. An old campaign with Gontrok ended in a dwarf-village atrocity, exile, and a long stretch raising orphaned children—a story he keeps until Gontrok forces it into the open.

He announces himself with a practice bout broadcast to the whole area, spars a stillwater under a no-magic rule, and loses a one-spell match to Diane, who lets him live. He steps between Kate and a mob, honors a guide deal without taking pay, and takes a shortcut into sacred woods. Collared into dwarf form in the ruins and mines, he still breaks cells and hydra floors, then asks before the next smash. When the collars come off, he chooses to stay with the group.

City squalor and arena spectacle suit him better than pinecone hunts. He clears a terrified division with an empty bottle, tames a griffin rather than finish it, and dives on a dragon as an eagle. He punches tunnels instead of finding levers, glues a mammoth’s trunk so someone else can hit the weak point, and names that kind of fight as why he joined. A Time-God trial finishes early; he will not say what was in it.

Underground, paperwork and goblin gates try his patience. Gontrok finds him again; Hadrik trains new size-shifts, claims the duel through a street fight, and is left without a last hit when the other man teleports out. Later Gontrok dies offstage in a way Hadrik calls dishonorable even for an enemy. Magium lectures make him want a drink and a simpler question. In the ether he struggles to keep thoughts quiet; on the king-and-Peter alliance route, Flower returns his mind in time for him to smash an Overseer core.

He walks out of a plan to kill Eiden while weak, then takes a revised deal because he was staying for adventure anyway. He befriends a guard with ale contests, refuses to detour around a real fight, stands with Kate when Diane is named, and charges a stillwater at full power with a grin.
